CPG 2.3           Asthma

Patient Care Goals
   1.   Conduct a rapid assessment focusing on airway, breathing, circulation, and disability (ABCD).
   2.   Perform respiratory assessment to identify severity.
   3.   Assure adequate oxygenation and ventilation.
   4.   Recognize impending respiratory failure.
   5.   Promptly identify and intervene for patients who require escalation of therapy.
   6.   Alleviate respiratory distress.

Patient Inclusion & Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion Criteria
    • Patients with mild, moderate, severe, or life-threatening asthma based on respiratory assessment findings.
Exclusion Criteria
    • Patients presenting with difficulty of breathing due to another cause (e.g. COPD, bronchiolitis, pulmonary edema).


Key Patient Assessment Points
   1. Patient Assessment
           • Conduct a primary and secondary survey as per Universal Care CPG.
   2. Patient Monitoring
           • Attach SpO2 monitor and ECG to all patients to continuously monitor oxygen saturation and cardiac rhythm
             throughout the assessment and treatment process.
           • Add NC-EtCO2 (via Nasal Cannula) monitoring for all moderate to severe and life-threatening cases.
   3. Respiratory Assessment
           • Perform a respiratory assessment, including:
                 • Auscultation: Listen for wheezes, silent chest, or diminished breath sounds.
                 • Observation: Assess respiratory rate, effort, and use of accessory muscles.
                 • Palpation: Evaluate the chest for deformities or tenderness.
                 • Assess for signs of severe or life-threatening asthma: silent chest, Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) <14,
                   blood pressure <90/60 mmHg, or very slow/gasping breathing.

Patient Management & Interventions
   1. Consider ALS back-up
          • Consider the requirement for ALS back-up as per Universal Care CPG.
   2. Oxygen Therapy
          • If a nebulizer is not available, and SpO2 <94%, administer oxygen therapy via a non-rebreather mask (NRB)
            to maintain SpO2 between 94-98%.
   2. Mild Asthma
          • Administer Nebulized Salbutamol:
                 • Adult: Salbutamol 5mg Neb.
                 • Pediatric (<5 years): 2.5mg Neb.
                 • Pediatric (>5 years): 5mg Neb.
                 • Repeat after 15 minutes if required.
                 • Dilute nebulized Salbutamol with Normal Saline or Sterile Water to a maximum of 5ml.
   3. Moderate Asthma
          • Administer treatment as per mild asthma, and:
          • Administer Nebulized Combivent:
                 • Adults: 0.5mg Ipratropium Bromide and 2.5mg Salbutamol, Nebulized.
                     • May be repeated after 20minutes if required. Maximum 3 doses.
                 • Pediatric: Not recommended for pediatric administration.
          • Administer Hydrocortisone:
                 • Adults: Hydrocortisone 100mg IV/IM.
                 • Pediatric (<1 year): 25mg IV/IM.
                 • Pediatric (1-5 years): 50mg IV/IM.
                 • Pediatric (>5 years): 100mg IV/IM.
   4. Severe Asthma
          • Administer treatment as per moderate asthma, and:
          • Request ALS Back-Up.
          • Administer Magnesium Sulphate
                 • Adults: 2g IV/IM. Administer as an infusion over 20 minutes.
                 • Pediatric: 40mg/kg IV/IM, maximum 2g, Administer as an infusion over 20 minutes.
   5. Life-Threatening Asthma
          • Administer treatment as per severe asthma, and:
          • Administer Adrenaline IM
                 • Adults: IM 0.3-0.5mg
                 • Pediatric: IM 0.15-0.3mg.
          • Administer IV Adrenaline
                 • Adults: IV infusion 2-10mcg/min.
                 • Pediatric: IV infusion 0.1-1mcg/kg/min.
   6. Patient Reassessment
          • Frequently Reassess the patient as per Universal Care CPG.

Patient Safety Considerations
   • Use a syringe driver for IV infusions when available.
   • It is not recommended to administer IV adrenaline infusions without a syringe driver to adults and pediatrics.

Clinical Insights & Additional Guidance
   • Call for back-up early.
         • Severe and life-threatening asthmatics are at high risk of deterioration and respiratory arrest.
   • Hydrocortisone
         • Hydrocortisone is preferred to be given IV in adults with severe asthma.
         • Hydrocortisone in severe asthma can be increased to:
                  • Adults: 200mg.
                  • Pediatrics: 4mg/kg.
         • If a patient has deteriorated into the severe or life-threatening category, and hydrocortisone has already been
           administered, it is not a priority to administer a "top-up" dose.
   • IV Adrenaline:
         • IV adrenaline should be administered via a syringe driver where possible.
         • Careful patient assessment should be undertaken prior to increasing the IV adrenaline administration rate to
           determine the difference between worsening asthma and the effects of IV adrenaline.
         • IV Adrenaline administration requires careful monitoring, especially in pediatrics, using a syringe driver and
           assess for worsening asthma vs. adrenaline effects.

Documentation
   • Identification: Full patient name, age, and gender.
   • Mechanism / Medical Complaint: Presenting symptoms (e.g., wheezing, respiratory distress), initial respiratory
     status.
   • Injuries / Information: Relevant history (e.g., asthma triggers, allergies).
   • Signs:
         • Full vital signs including pulse, respiratory rate, SpO2, EtCO2.
         • Two sets of vital signs unless critical patient condition precludes.
         • Respiratory assessment findings (e.g., wheeze, accessory muscle use).
   • Treatment:
         • Oxygen administration and nebulized medication doses.
         • All interventions: Salbutamol, Combivent, Hydrocortisone, Magnesium Sulphate, Adrenaline, and response.
         • Justification if interventions were withheld.
   • Allergies: Documented allergies and drug interactions.
   • Medications: All pre-existing medications and any administered in the field.
   • Background: Relevant medical history, comorbidities, and recent procedures.
   • Other:
         • Communication with Telemedicine (time and instructions).
         • Response to interventions.
         • Consideration of reversible causes and actions taken.


Key Performance Indicators
   1. Document respiratory assessment findings.
   2. Administer treatment (e.g. salbutamol) for moderate to life threatening cases within 10 minutes of EMS arrival.
