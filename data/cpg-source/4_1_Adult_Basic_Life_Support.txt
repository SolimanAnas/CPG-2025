CPG 4.1           Adult Basic Life Support

Patient Care Goals
   1. Rapidly identify and initiate high-quality cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) for adults in cardiac arrest.
   2. Ensure ALS back-up is requested.
   3. Rapidly apply AED (if available) and deliver a shock .

Patient Inclusion & Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion Criteria
    • Adult patients (≥13 years) presenting with cardiac arrest, including:
        • Unresponsiveness with no signs of life (e.g., no pulse, no breathing, or agonal gasps).
        • Suspected pulseless electrical activity (PEA), ventricular fibrillation (VF), ventricular tachycardia (VT), or
          asystole.
    • Patients requiring immediate BLS interventions, including chest compressions, ventilations, and automated external
      defibrillator (AED) use.
Exclusion Criteria
    • Patients with obvious signs of irreversible death (e.g., rigor mortis, dependent lividity, or decapitation; refer to
        Verification of Death CPG).
    • Pediatric patients (<13 years).

Key Patient Assessment Points
   1. Cardiac Arrest Confirmation
         • Verify unresponsiveness by assessing for no response to verbal or painful stimuli (AVPU).
         • Check for absence of pulse (carotid) for 5-10 seconds.
         • Confirm absence of normal breathing or presence of agonal gasps for 5-10 seconds.
   2. Scene and Bystander Assessment
         • Ensure scene safety (e.g., no electrical or environmental hazards) before initiating BLS.
         • Determine if bystander CPR was initiated and its quality (e.g., rate, depth of compressions).
   3. Determine Triage Category
         • Determine Triage Category as per Patient Triage Categories CPG.
             • All unconscious patients and patients in cardiac arrest are Red Priority.


Patient Management & Interventions
   1. Check for Responsiveness
          • Verify unresponsiveness by assessing for no response to verbal or painful stimuli (AVPU).
   2. Call for ALS back-up
   3. Retrieve Emergency Equipment
          • First rescuer remains with the patient.
          • Second rescuer retrieves the AED and other emergency equipment.
   4. Primary Survey
          • Perform Head Tilt/Chin lift (jaw thrust for suspected trauma patients)
          • Look for no breathing or only gasping and check pulse (simultaneously).
          • Is pulse felt within 10 seconds?
   5. No breathing or only gasping. No pulse felt
          • Start CPR
          • First rescuer performs cycles of 30 compressions and 2 breaths.
          • Use AED as soon as it is available.
   6. No Normal Breathing. Pulse Felt
          • Provide rescue breathing, 1 breath every 6 seconds. (or 10 breaths/min)
          • Check pulse every 2 minutes;
               • If no pulse, start CPR.
          • If possible opioid overdose, go to Overdose/Poisoning CPG.
   7. Apply AED

Patient Safety Considerations
   • None noted


Clinical Insights & Additional Information
   • Chest Compressions
       • Initiate high-quality compressions immediately (100-120/min, 5-6 cm depth, full recoil).
       • Rotate compressors every 2 minutes to prevent fatigue and maintain quality.
       • Minimize interruptions (<10 seconds for rhythm checks or ventilations).
   • Ventilations
       • Provide ventilations via bag-valve-mask (BVM) at 10-12 breaths/min (1 breath every 5-6 seconds) with adequate
        chest rise.
      • Use two-person BVM technique if available to improve seal and ventilation.
   • Airway Management
       • Use oropharyngeal or nasopharyngeal airway if needed to maintain patency in unresponsive patients.
       • Suction secretions or vomit to prevent airway obstruction.
   • Oxygen
       • Administer 100% oxygen via BVM to optimize oxygenation during CPR.


Documentation
   • Identification: Patient’s full name, gender, and age.
   • Mechanism / Medical Complaint: Description of collapse or arrest, witness status, bystander CPR involvement.
   • Injuries / Information: Initial ECG rhythm, suspected cause of arrest (e.g., MI, respiratory failure, trauma).
   • Signs: Initial assessment findings, absence of breathing/pulse, ROSC indicators, capnography if available.
   • Treatment:
       • Time CPR initiated, AED applied, and defibrillation administered.
       • Ventilation method and rate (e.g., BVM with OPA, 10-12 breaths/min).
       • Use of airway adjuncts, suctioning, oxygen administration.
   • Allergies: Known allergies if information is available.
   • Medications: Pre-arrest medications if known (e.g., insulin, beta blockers).
   • Background: Any relevant past medical history from bystanders, family, or records.
   • Other: Scene observations, handover time, ALS arrival, team member roles, witness details.
       • Timestamp all key actions: CPR start, defibrillation, ROSC.
       • Two full sets of vital signs post-ROSC (if applicable).
   • ROSC documentation must include vitals, GCS, and interventions applied.


Key Performance Indicators
   1. Initiate chest compressions within 1 minute of recognition of cardiac arrest.
   2. Apply the AED within 3 minutes of recognition of cardiac arrest.
