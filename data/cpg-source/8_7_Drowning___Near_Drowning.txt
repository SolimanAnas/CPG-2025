CPG 8.7           Drowning

Patient Care Goals
   1. Ensure airway, oxygenation, and ventilation as top priorities
   2. Treat hypoxia aggressively — it is the primary cause of cardiac arrest in drowning
   3. Prevent aspiration-related complications (e.g., pulmonary edema, ARDS)
   4. Minimize time to BLS/ALS resuscitation in cardiac arrest
   5. Prevent hypothermia and monitor for its progression
   6. Provide safe transport and hospital handover, even in asymptomatic patients

Patient Inclusion & Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion Criteria
    • Any patient with a history of water immersion/submersion, including:
          • Unconscious, and/or apneic and/or hypoxic, and/or in cardiac arrest, and/or symptomatic after water
            submersion.
          • Pediatric or adult drowning events
Exclusion Criteria
    • Isolated trauma without water immersion
    • Medical cardiac arrest not related to drowning
    • Drowning confirmed >20 min submersion with signs of rigor mortis, dependent lividity, or decomposition


Key Patient Assessment Points
   1. Patient History
        • Time Submerged:
               • Ask or estimate duration of submersion, this is a critical prognostic indicator.
               • Longer submersion (>10 minutes) is associated with worse outcomes
        • Mechanism of Injury:
               • Assess for mechanism which may require spinal motion restriction.
   2. Airway
        • Is the airway open and protected?
        • Obstruction due to vomit, water, foam, or secretions is common.
        • Listen for gurgling or stridor.
        • Use suction if needed.
   3. Breathing
        • Assess respiratory rate, effort, and chest rise.
        • Monitor SpO₂, values <94% are abnormal even if the patient appears stable.
        • Auscultate for wheezing, crackles, or decreased air entry; may indicate aspiration or pulmonary edema.
        • Tachypnea or bradypnea indicating fatigue or failure.
   4. Circulation
        • Check heart rate, blood pressure, capillary refill, and skin color.
        • Bradycardia may be due to hypoxia or hypothermia, especially in children.
        • Palpate central and peripheral pulses.
   5. Disability (Neurological)
        • Use AVPU or GCS to assess level of consciousness.
        • Check for confusion, lethargy, or unresponsiveness.
        • Monitor for seizures in pediatric cases or post-resuscitation.
   6. Exposure & Environment
        • Check for hypothermia: cold skin, shivering, or decreased responsiveness. For further managment see
          Hypothermia CPG.
        • Remove wet clothing and protect from further heat loss.
        • Blood glucose: check in all patients with altered LOC; hypoglycemia may complicate presentation.
   7. Determine Triage Category
        • Determine Triage Category as per Patient Triage Categories CPG.

        • Red Priority

              • SpO₂ <90% despite oxygen
              • Ongoing respiratory distress or abnormal lung sounds (e.g., crackles, wheezing)
              • Bradycardia, hypotension, or signs of shock

        • Yellow Priority
              • Coughing, vomiting, or mild respiratory symptoms with normal vitals
              • Alert and oriented but with history of submersion
              • Minor wheezing or crackles but SpO₂ ≥94% on room air
        • Green Priority
              • No symptoms, full recovery on scene, normal exam


Patient Management & Interventions
   1. Consider ALS back-up
         • Consider the requirement for ALS back-up as per Universal Care CPG.
         • ALS back-up should be routinely requested for:
             • Altered LOC, respiratory distress, or requires advanced airway management.
             • Cardiac arrest present or ROSC achieved
             • Signs of pulmonary edema or suspected aspiration pneumonia
   2. Primary Survey
         • Perform and Manage Primary Survey as per Universal Care CPG.
         • Airway & Ventilation
         • Suctioning of the airway be required to clear water, vomitus, or foam
         • For unconscious patients, consider airway adjuncts
   3. Administer oxygen
         • Via NRB or BVM as required.
         • Titrate oxygen to maintain Spo2 >94%
   4. Consider CPAP / BiPaP
         • Indications:
             • Conscious patient with Moderate to Severe respiratory difficulty.
   5. If No Pulse or No Normal Breathing
         • Prioritize 5 initial rescue breaths before starting compressions.
         • Perform standard CPR as per Adult or Pediatric Cardiac Arrest CPG.
   6. Spinal Precautions (Spinal Motion Restriction)
         • Routine Spinal motion restriction is not required.
         • Spinal Motion Restriction is only required if traumatic mechanism (such as shallow diving accident)
   7. Hypothermia
         • Rewarming if Hypothermic
         • For further management see Hypothermia CPG.
   8. Blood Glucose
         • Check early in all unconscious or lethargic patients
         • Treat as per Hypoglycemia CPG if glucose low.
   9. Transport
         • Transport all symptomatic patients, even if improved on scene
         • Prioritize high-risk patients: long submersion, persistent cough, altered LOC, low SpO₂, pediatric.
         • Asymptomatic patients after minor events may be treated at scene with advice to call back or visit emergency
           department.
   10. Patient Reassessment
         • Frequently Reassess the patient as per Universal Care CPG.
         • Specific Patient Reassessment Points:

Patient Safety Considerations
   • Prioritize oxygenation over rapid extrication unless unsafe scene
   • Handle hypothermic patients gently to avoid arrhythmias
   • Do not delay resuscitation for spinal precautions unless trauma is suspected
   • Children may appear stable initially and deteriorate; transport all pediatric cases with symptoms
   • Monitor closely for recurrent hypoxia or pulmonary edema


Clinical Insights & Additional Guidance
   • Airway and Ventilation Management:
         • Airway Management and ventilation should be early and aggressive.
         • The most serious problem in drowning is lack of oxygen (hypoxia), not the amount of water in the lungs. Even a
          small amount of water can cause lung irritation and inhibit oxygenation.
        • Do NOT attempt to expel water from lungs (Heimlich/abdominal thrusts), this is ineffective and delays
          resuscitation.
   • Salbutamol
         • Nebulized Salbutamol may be administered if fluid is auscultated, however the priority for management of
          patients is on the priority measures noted above.
        • Where possible, Salbutamol should be administered via the CPAP nebulizer.
        • The doses of Salbutamol for adults and pediatrics are the same as in asthma.
   • Transportation Guidance:
         • Patients may initially recover but develop delayed pulmonary edema or ARDS. Transportation to hospital
          should be recommended.
        • Always advise caregivers of warning signs for deterioration if not transported.
        • Consult with TeleEMS if clarification is required for borderline or asymptomatic cases
   • Pediatrics
        • Pediatric patients have better outcomes even after prolonged submersion; initiate full resuscitation


Documentation
   • Identification: Age, sex, weight
   • Medical complaint: Drowning or near-drowning
   • Information: Submersion time, water type, time found, bystander CPR?
   • Signs: RR, SpO₂, HR, BP, GCS, skin color, temperature
   • Treatment: Oxygen, suction, airway, CPR, ALS involvement
   • Allergies: Known drug or environmental allergies
   • Medications: Any meds administered or found at scene
   • Background: Medical history, seizures, trauma, cardiac history
   • Other: Time of event, ALS/TeleEMS consulted, crew handover


Key Performance Indicators
   1. Investigate and document submersion time.
   2. Request ALS back-up for red priority patients with a transfer time of >10 minutes.
