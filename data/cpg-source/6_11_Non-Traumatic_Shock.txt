CPG 6.11          Non Traumatic Shock

Patient Care Goals
   1. Identification and management of life-threatening non-traumatic shock (e.g., hypovolemic, cardiogenic,
      distributive, or obstructive).
   2. Improve the patient’s hemodynamic status through administration of fluids, vasopressors, and supportive care.

Patient Inclusion & Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion Criteria
    • Patients presenting with signs and symptoms of shock which is not related to trauma, including:
          • Hypotension (SBP <90 mmHg in adults, or age-specific for pediatrics).
          • Tachycardia (HR >100 in adults, or age-specific for pediatrics).
          • Poor perfusion (e.g., cool, clammy skin, delayed capillary refill >2 seconds).
          • Altered mental status, weakness, or respiratory distress.
    • Suspected underlying causes such as sepsis, anaphylaxis, cardiogenic shock, or internal bleeding (e.g.,
      gastrointestinal or ectopic pregnancy).
Exclusion Criteria
    • Shock clearly attributable to trauma.


Key Patient Assessment Points
   1. Shock Type Identification
       • Assess for clues to differentiate shock types:
             • Hypovolemic: History of fluid loss (e.g., vomiting, diarrhea, or occult bleeding).
             • Cardiogenic: Chest pain, dyspnea, or history of heart disease.
             • Distributive: Signs of sepsis (e.g., fever, infection) or anaphylaxis (e.g., urticaria, wheezing).
             • Obstructive: Symptoms suggestive of pulmonary embolism or pericardial tamponade (e.g., jugular vein
               distension, muffled heart sounds).
   2. Perfusion Status
       • Evaluate skin temperature, color, and moisture (e.g., cool, pale, or mottled skin).
       • Assess for signs of organ dysfunction (e.g., altered mental status, decreased urine output).
   3. Determine Triage Category
       • Determine Triage Category as per Patient Triage Categories CPG.


Patient Management & Interventions
   1. Primary and Secondary Survey
        • Perform primary and secondary surveys as per Universal Care CPG.
        • Consider Oxygen
              • Titrate to maintain SpO2 ≥94% if hypoxia or respiratory distress is present.
   2. Consider Red Flags
        • Persistent hypotension (SBP <90 mmHg in adults, or age-specific for pediatrics) despite fluid resuscitation.
        • Severe altered mental status or unresponsiveness.
        • Hypoxia (SpO2 <90%) or respiratory failure.
        • Cardiogenic or Obstructive shock.
   3. Evaluate for Signs of Shock or Instability
        • Hypotension, tachycardia, or poor perfusion (e.g., cool, clammy skin, delayed capillary refill >2 seconds).
   4. Consider ALS back-up
        • Consider the requirement for ALS back-up as per Universal Care CPG.
   5. Establish IV Access
        • Indicated for hypotension, suspected shock, or anticipated medication/fluid administration.
        • Consider intraosseous (IO) access if IV access cannot be obtained.
   6. Consider Condition-Specific Treatment
        • For Septic and Anaphylactic Shock, refer to appropriate CPG for additional treatment considerations and
          additional shock management.

   7. Fluid Resuscitation
        • Fluid Administration Targets:
                • Fluid bolus’ should be administered to target:
                    • Adults: MAP (mean arterial pressure) = 65mmHg
                    • Pediatrics: Systolic blood pressure of at least fifth percentile for age, strong distal pulses, warm
                      skin perfusion, capillary refill less than 2 seconds and improving mental status.
        • Administer Normal Saline or Ringers Lactate:
              • Adults: 250-500 mL bolus IV, repeat as needed (up to 2000ml)
              • Pediatrics: 20 mL/kg bolus IV, repeat as needed (max 40 mL/kg).
        • Use caution in obstructive/cardiogenic shock; administer smaller boluses.
   8. Consider Vasopressors
        • Indications:
              • Fluid refractory hypotension after fluid resuscitation (once fluid maximums have been reached).
              • Hypotension with Pulmonary Edema (e.g. Obstructive/Cardiogenic shock).
        • Dopamine
              • Adults: 5-20 mcg/kg/min IV infusion.
              • Pediatrics: 5-20 mcg/kg/min IV infusion.
   9. Patient Reassessment
        • Frequently Reassess the patient as per Universal Care CPG.


Patient Safety Considerations
   • Elderly Patients
         • Shock may present atypically (e.g., confusion, minimal tachycardia due to beta-blocker use).
         • Use caution with fluid boluses in patients with heart failure; consult Tele EMS for guidance.
   • Fluid Resuscitation:
         • Avoid over-resuscitation in cardiogenic or obstructive shock; monitor for signs of pulmonary edema (e.g.,
           crackles, dyspnea).

Clinical Insights & Additional Guidance
   • Common Causes:
         • Hypovolemic: Dehydration, gastrointestinal bleeding, or ruptured ectopic pregnancy.
         • Cardiogenic: Myocardial infarction, heart failure, or arrhythmias.
         • Distributive: Sepsis, anaphylaxis, or neurogenic shock (e.g., spinal cord injury).
         • Obstructive: Pulmonary embolism, pericardial tamponade, or tension pneumothorax.
   • Possible Alternative Causes:
         • Traumatic shock, adrenal insufficiency, hypoglycemia, or drug overdose (e.g., antihypertensives).


Documentation
   • Identification: Full patient name, age, and gender.
   • Mechanism / Medical Complaint: Primary complaint or symptoms such as dizziness, syncope, hypotension, or
     chest pain.
   • Injuries / Information: Relevant history including recent illness, known allergies, chronic diseases, and medication
     use.
   • Signs: Vital signs (BP, HR, RR, SpO₂), mental status, skin condition (pale, cool, clammy), capillary refill, GCS or
     AVPU.
   • Treatment:
         • Oxygen therapy: rate, method, response.
         • IV fluid administration: type, volume, and response.
         • Vasopressors if used: name, dose, and response.
   • Allergies: Drug or substance allergies relevant to medications administered.
   • Medications: Current or recent medications including antihypertensives, diuretics, or vasodilators.
