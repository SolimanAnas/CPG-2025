CPG 3.3           Adult Tachycardia with a Pulse

Patient Care Goals
   1. Identify patients with tachycardia requiring intervention.
   2. Identification of underlying causes.
   3. Restoration normal rhythm and hemodynamic stability.

Patient Inclusion & Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion Criteria
    • Adult patients presenting with tachycardia and a palpable pulse (typically >150 bpm).
    • Conscious and unconscious adult patients
Exclusion Criteria
    • Tachycardia with a suspected non-cardiac cause which may require other treatment (e.g. sepsis or other
      physiological causes).
    • Pediatric patients <13 years.


Key Patient Assessment Points
   1. 12-Lead ECG
   2. Identification of cardiac rhythm
         • Such as: VT, SVT, Sinus Tachycardia, other.
   3. Identification and Treatment of other causes:
         • Consider toxins/drugs.
         • Physiological Causes
                • Sepsis
                • Pain/anxiety
                • Hypovolaemia
                • Anaemia
                • Street drugs
                • Hyperthyroidism
   4. Determine Triage Category
         • Determine Triage Category as per Patient Triage Categories CPG.
         • Red Priority:
              • Ventricular Tachycardia (VT)
              • Supraventricular Tachycardia (SVT)
              • Dysrhythmia with Signs of Severe Shock or hypotension


Patient Management & Interventions
   1. Perform Primary Survey
          • As per Universal Care CPG.
   2. Attach Cardiac Monitor
          • Assess Vital Signs
          • Assess underlying cardiac rhythm
          • Perform 12-Lead ECG
              • Performing a 12-Lead ECG may not be an immediate priority in unconscious patients or patients who
                have significant issues with their primary survey, or have a life-threatening arrhythmia.
   3. Consider ALS back-up
          • Consider the requirement for ALS back-up as per Universal Care CPG.
   4. Identify and treat underlying causes
   5. Establish IV Access
          • Consider IO access if gaining IV access may be delayed or is otherwise not feasible.
   6. Assess for Signs of Instability
          • Hypotension
          • Acutely altered mental state

   7. If Patient is Hemodynamically Unstable
           • Request for ALS back-up
           • Consider Adenosine
                  • 1st Dose: 6mg IV/IO, rapid injection, with 20ml Normal Saline Flush.
                  • 2nd Dose: 12mg IV/IO, rapid injection, with 20ml Normal Saline Flush.
                  • Indicated for:
                         • Regular tachycardias. And/
                         • Patients who are not significantly compromised, or/
                         • Where sedation is not available and cardioversion without sedation would be inappropriate.
           • Perform Synchronized Cardioversion
                  • Consider sedation
                  • Select Joule settings appropriate to the underlying rhythm. (see below).
                  • If rhythm does not revert:
                       • Increase Joule settings (see below) and perform synchronized cardioversion.
           • If the patient deteriorates into cardiac arrest, go to Cardiac Arrest CPG.
   8. If Patient is Hemodynamically Stable
           • Narrow Complex Tachycardia (HR > 150/min with QRS < 0.12 sec)
                  • Vagal Stimulation (If REGULAR)
                         • Preferred Method: Modified Valsalva.
                         • Alternative Methods: Valsalva, ice water applied to face, coughing, breath-holding.
                  • IV Fluid Bolus
                         • 250ml IV/IO crystalloid bolus.
                         • Reassess frequently for fluid overload. Stop administration if fluid overload presents.
                  • Adenosine (if REGULAR)
                         • 1st Dose: 6mg IV/IO, rapid injection, with 20ml Normal Saline Flush.
                         • 2nd Dose: 12mg IV/IO, rapid injection, with 20ml Normal Saline Flush.
           • Wide Complex Tachycardia (HR > 150/min with QRS > 0.12 sec)
                  • Adenosine (if REGULAR)
                         • 1st Dose: 6mg IV/IO, rapid injection, with 20ml Normal Saline Flush.
                         • 2nd Dose: 12mg IV/IO, rapid injection, with 20ml Normal Saline Flush.
                  • IV Fluid Bolus
                         • 250ml IV/IO crystalloid bolus.
                         • Reassess frequently for fluid overload. Stop administration if fluid overload presents.
                  • Amiodarone
                         • Amiodarone Infusion:
                             • 150mg infused over 10 minutes IV/IO. (15mg/min)
                             • Diluted in 5% dextrose in 100ml (D5W).
                         • Repeat dose (150mg) if rhythm fails to revert.
                         • Follow with a 1 mg/min infusion.
   9. If Torsades de Pointes
             • Consider Asynchronous Defibrillation
             • Administer Magnesium 2g IV over 10 min.
   10. If Rhythm is Refractory to interventions:
             • Consider underlying causes.
             • Consider Tele-EMS consultation.
   11. Patient Reassessment
             • Frequently Reassess the patient as per Universal Care CPG.

Patient Safety Considerations
   • Ensure equipment for cardiac arrest management is readily available during cardioversion.
   • Monitor for signs of fluid overload, especially with IV fluid administration.
   • Avoid vagal stimulation if varying R-R intervals or atrial fibrillation is present.


Clinical Insights & Additional Information
   • Preparing for Cardiac Arrest
       • Electrical and chemical cardioversion have a risk of patient deterioration into cardiac arrest. When cardioverting
         any patient, either with electricity or medication, equipment to manage cardiac arrest should be readily
         available.
   • Compromised / Unstable Patients
       • Synchronized Cardioversion is generally the first-line treatment.

Documentation
   • Identification: Document patient demographics and clinical condition.
   • Mechanism: Note suspected cause of tachycardia (e.g., shock, sepsis, hyperthyroidism).
   • Injuries/Illness: Record signs of instability (e.g., hypotension, altered mental state).
   • Signs/Symptoms: Include vital signs, ECG findings, and response to interventions.
   • Treatment: Detail medications (e.g., adenosine doses, amiodarone infusion), cardioversion attempts, and vagal
     manoeuvres.
   • Allergies: Confirm and document any known allergies.
   • Medications: List patient’s current medications.
   • Background: Provide medical history relevant to tachycardia.
   • Other: Note ALS back-up request, telemedicine consultation, and specialist advice sought.


Key Performance Indicators
   1. Perform synchronized cardioversion on all hemodynamically compromised patients.
