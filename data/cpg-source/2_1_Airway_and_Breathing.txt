CPG 2.1           Airway Management

Patient Care Goals
   1. Maintain a patent airway.
   2. Provide effective oxygenation and adequate ventilation using the most appropriate method to achieve those goals,
      paired with pulse oximetry and end-tidal capnography (EtCO2) data.
   3. Anticipate, recognize, and alleviate respiratory distress.
   4. Provide necessary interventions quickly and safely to patients with the need for respiratory support.
   5. Anticipate, identify, and plan for a potentially difficult airway.
   6. Optimize the patient for any advanced airway attempts, facilitating appropriate initial assessment and
      management, and linking to specific guidelines as dictated by findings.
   7. Achieve a target oxygen saturation of 92-98% to optimize oxygenation and minimize complications associated
      with hypoventilation.
   8. Ensure timely recognition and management of life-threatening conditions (e.g., cardiac arrest, severe trauma) that
      may require advanced airway techniques.

Patient Inclusion & Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion Criteria
    • Airway compromise identified during primary assessment, including:
            • Significantly altered mental status (GCS<8, or unresponsive on AVPU scale)
            • Noisy breathing
            • Stridor
            • Ineffective ventilation
            • Foreign body airway obstruction
Exclusion Criteria
    • Patients without identified airway compromise.


Key Patient Assessment Points
   1. Primary Survey
          • Conduct a primary survey as per Universal Care CPG.
          • Identify airway compromise: Look for signs of obstruction, including noisy breathing, stridor, or ineffective
            ventilation.
   2. Airway Assessment
          • Assess the following:
                • Mental status
                • Airway obstruction with foreign body or swelling (e.g., angioedema, posterior pharyngeal and laryngeal
                  infections).
                • Signs of a difficult airway (short jaw or limited jaw thrust or mobility, small thyromental space, upper
                  airway obstruction, large tongue, obesity, large tonsils, large neck, craniofacial abnormalities,
                  excessive facial hair, tracheostomy scar or evidence of other neck/facial surgery, trismus).
   3. Breathing Assessment
          • Assess the following:
                • Abnormal respiratory pattern, rate and/or effort.
                • Use of accessory muscles.
                • Ability to speak words/sentences.
                • Quality of air exchange, including depth of respiration and equality of breath sounds.
                • Abnormal breath sounds (e.g., wheezing, rhonchi, rales, or stridor).
                • Cough.
                • Skin color (cyanosis or pallor), presence of diaphoresis.
                • Anxiety.
                • Signs of fluid overload (e.g., ascites, peripheral edema).
                • Traumatic injuries impairing upper and lower airway anatomy and physiology:
                       • Facial injuries.

Patient Management & Interventions
   1. Primary Survey
          • Conduct a primary survey as per Universal Care CPG.
   2. Determine Triage Category
          • Determine Triage Category as per Patient Triage Categories CPG.
   3. Perform Basic Airway Management
          • Clear airway: Remove any foreign body, vomitus, or blood using suction or Magill’s forceps where indicated –
            refer to the Foreign Body Airway Obstruction (FBAO) CPG.
          • Attach monitors (SpO2, EtCO2, ECG) and initiate continuous monitoring to assess cardiac rhythm and
            oxygenation status.
   4. Oxygen Therapy
          • Administer Oxygen as needed to maintain SpO2 >94%.
   5. Continue Basic Airway Management
          • Ensure airway patency: Use head-tilt/chin-lift or jaw-thrust maneuvers as appropriate, avoiding neck
            manipulation if cervical injury is suspected.
          • Clear airway: Remove visible obstructions (e.g., foreign bodies, blood, secretions) using suction or Magill’s
            forceps where indicated – refer to the Foreign Body Airway Obstruction (FBAO) CPG.
          • Maintain airway patency: Use oropharyngeal airway (OPA) or nasopharyngeal airway (NPA) as appropriate,
            ensuring proper size and avoiding complications (e.g., gagging, bleeding).
          • Provide bag-valve-mask (BVM) ventilation with 100% oxygen if spontaneous breathing is inadequate or
            ineffective, ensuring a good mask seal and effective ventilation sounds.
   6. Consider ALS back-up
          • Consider the requirement for ALS back-up as per Universal Care CPG.
          • Consider requesting ALS back-up for advanced airway management if:
                • The patient is in cardiac arrest or deeply unconscious (no gag reflex anticipated).
                • Severe respiratory distress or impending respiratory failure.
                • A foreign body is observed in the airway and cannot be removed.
   7. Consider Supraglottic Airway (LT Airway)
          • Indications:
                • Cardiac Arrest
                • Patient is profoundly unconscious AND basic airway adjuncts (NPA/OPA) fail to maintain airway.
          • Note: EMT’s may only insert supraglottic airways (LT Airways) in cardiac arrest.
          • Confirm successful LT airway placement with waveform capnography (EtCO2).
          • Select LT size and LT cuff inflation volume according to colour coded labels on the syringe.

   8. Consider: CPAP / BiPaP
           • Indications
                  • Acute Pulmonary Edema
                  • Moderate to severe COPD unresponsive to initial nebulizer treatments.
           • Cautions:
                  • Severe pneumonia / ARDS (increase likelihood of pneumothorax)
                  • Emphysema (increased pressure may rupture lung bullae)
                  • Asthma (may further increase dynamic hyperinflation)
           • Absolute Contraindications
                  • Unconscious or altered mental status
                  • Apnea
                  • Facial Trauma or deformities (unable to gain adequate mask seal)
                  • Pneumothorax (untreated)
                  • Hypotension
   9. Consider: Endotracheal Intubation
           • Indications:
                  • Cardiac Arrest (see Cardiac Arrest CPG for further guidance on Endotracheal Intubation)
                  • Patient is profoundly unconscious AND basic airway adjuncts (NPA/OPA) fail to maintain airway.
           • Ensure equipment and team readiness prior to performing the procedure.
           • If Successful Intubation:
                  • Confirm ET tube placement with EtC02 waveform capnography.
                  • Connect the bag-valve to the tube and provide ventilations at 1 breath every 6 seconds or as clinically
                    indicated.
                  • Connect to a ventilator where available – refer the Invasive Ventilation CPG.
                  • Assess airway patency, oxygenation (SpO2), and ventilation (EtCO2) using attached monitors and
                    capnography.
           • If Failed Intubation:
                  • Consider a second attempt with ET or supraglottic device, ensuring minimal delay.
                      • Ensure optimal airway positioning and vocal cord visualization prior to a second attempt.
           • If the Second Attempt fails:
                  • Revert to airway management using supraglottic device (LT airway) or OPA/NPA.
                  • Consider consulting Tele-EMS for alternative airway strategies.
   10. Patient Reassessment
           • Frequently Reassess the patient as per Universal Care CPG.
           • Specific Patient Reassessment Points:
                • Airway Reassessment
                  • Continuously reassess the patient’s airway status, breathing, and response to treatment using
                    attached monitors (SpO2, ECG, EtCO2).
                  • Reassess the placement of supraglottic airway (LT) and Endotracheal Tubes after each patient
                    movement. (e.g. from ground to stretcher and from stretcher to hospital bed).
   11. Support Ventilation
           • Administer 15 L/min oxygen via NRB mask or BVM as required.
                  • BVM/Supported Ventilation Rates:
                      • Adult: 10-12 b/min.
                      • Child: 20-30 b/min.
                      • Infant: 20-30 b/min.
                      • Continuously monitor EtCO2 for tidal volume and minute ventilation.


Patient Safety Considerations

        • Use attached monitors (SpO2, EtCO2, ECG) to assess ventilation and oxygenation.

Documentation
   • Complete a comprehensive electronic Patient Care Record (ePCR), capturing the following elements to support
     continuity of care. Including:
           • Identification: Full patient name, age, and gender.
           • Mechanism / Medical Complaint: Presenting symptoms (e.g., noisy breathing, stridor), initial airway status.
           • Injuries / Information: Relevant history (e.g., trauma, burns, allergies).
           • Signs:
               • Full vital signs including pulse, blood pressure, respiratory rate, SpO2, EtCO2.
               • Two sets of vital signs unless critical patient condition precludes.
               • Airway assessment findings (e.g., patency, obstruction).
           • Treatment:
               • Airway management steps and oxygen administration.
               • All interventions: OPA/NPA, BVM, advanced airway attempts, and response.
               • Justification if advanced airway was withheld.
           • Allergies: Documented allergies and drug interactions.
           • Medications: All pre-existing medications and any administered in the field.
           • Background: Relevant medical history, comorbidities, and recent procedures.
           • Other:
               • Communication with Tele-EMS (time and instructions).
               • Response to interventions.
               • Consideration of reversible causes and actions taken.


Key Performance Indicators
   1. Document initial airway findings on all patients with airway compromise.
   2. Administer oxygen to all patients with SpO₂ <92% (when appropriate).
   3. Intubation should be successful on the first intubation attempt.
