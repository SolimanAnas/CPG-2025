CPG 11.1           S.T.A.R.T Triage (MCI Triage)

Patient Care Goals
    1. Rapidly categorize patients in mass casualty incidents (MCIs) to prioritize treatment and resource allocation
       based on injury severity and survivability.
    2. Identify life-threatening conditions quickly to facilitate immediate interventions while maximizing the number of
       survivors.
    3. Ensure efficient scene management and transport decisions based on patient priority.

Patient Inclusion & Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion Criteria
    • Patients involved in mass casualty incidents (e.g., multi-vehicle accidents, explosions, natural disasters) requiring
      triage.
    • All individuals at the scene, regardless of age, presenting with potential injuries or exposure.
    • Symptomatic or asymptomatic patients needing rapid assessment for prioritization.
Exclusion Criteria
    • Patients not part of a MCI.


Key Patient Assessment Points
    1. Sequential Evaluation (START Algorithm)
            • Is the Patient Breathing Spontaneously?
                   • Yes: Continue to assess Respiratory Rate
                   • No: Position Airway, and reassess for Spontaneous Breathing:
                         • No Spontaneous Breathing: Categorize as Expectant (Black)
                         • Spontaneous Breathing: Continue to assess Respiratory Rate
            • Respiratory Rate Assessment:
                   • Respiratory Rate > 30: Categorize as Immediate (Red)
                   • Respiratory Rate < 30: Continue to Perfusion Assessment.
            • Perfusion Assessment:
                   • Radial Pulse Absent or Capillary Refill >2seconds: Categorize as Immediate (Red)
                   • Radial Pulse Present or Capillary Refill <2seconds: Continue to Mental Status Assessment.
            • Mental Status Assessment:
                   • Doesn’t obey commands: Categorize as Immediate (Red)
                   • Obeys commands: Categorize as Delayed (Yellow)
    2. Category/Priority Assignment
        • Immediate (Red): Requires urgent intervention.
        • Delayed (Yellow): Serious but not immediately life-threatening.
        • Minor (Green): Walking wounded; minimal injuries.
        • Expectant (Black): Unlikely to survive given resources.


Adult Patient Management & Interventions
    2. Start with Global Command:
         • Start with global command:
                • "If you can hear me and are able to walk, come to my voice."
         • Categorize all walking/ambulatory patients as Green Priority.
         • Direct green priority patients to a designated area.
    3. For non-ambulatory:
         • Apply basic interventions during triage: Airway positioning for apnea; control massive hemorrhage if identified.
         • Follow Sequential Evaluation
             • Is the Patient Breathing Spontaneously?
                     • Yes: Continue to assess Respiratory Rate
                     • No: Position Airway, and reassess for Spontaneous Breathing:
                           • No Spontaneous Breathing: Categorize as Expectant (Black)

Patient Safety Considerations
   • Scene Safety:
        • Prioritize personal protective equipment; avoid entering unsafe areas during initial triage.


Clinical Insights & Additional Guidance
   • Applying a triage tag to each patient is essential to ensure patients are treated in order of priority and to provide
     effective scene management.

Documentation
   • Triage Tag Category Assigned should be documented by transferring team.

Key Performance Indicators
   1. Complete triage assessment within 30 seconds (per patient)
   2. Apply a triage tag to all patients triaged.
