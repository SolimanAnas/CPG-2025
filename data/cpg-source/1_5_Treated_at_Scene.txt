CPG 1.5            Treated at Scene

Patient Care Goals
    1. Safely and effectively recommend non-ambulance transfer for patients with minor or stable conditions.
    2. Ensure informed decision-making regarding the non-transfer of patients balancing patient autonomy and clinical
       stability.
    3. Document all assessments, interventions, and patient education to support continuity of care and legal
       compliance.

Patient Inclusion & Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion Criteria
    • Patients with minor or stable medical conditions that can be safely managed and resolved at scene, including:
            • Mild allergic reactions (e.g., localized hives resolved with antihistamines).
            • Minor injuries (e.g., superficial abrasions, small lacerations with controlled bleeding).
            • Minor musculoskeletal pain (without deformity or reduced function)
            • Stable medical complaints (e.g., mild hypoglycemia corrected with oral glucose, resolved nausea).
            • Mild resolved asthma symptoms (no wheeze, normal SpO₂)
            • Isolated low-grade fever in adults with normal vitals.
    • Patients with no red flags, stable vital signs, and capacity to make informed decisions about remaining at scene.
    • Patients with access to follow-up care (e.g., primary care, pharmacy) and/or a responsible caregiver.
Exclusion Criteria
    • Patients with unstable conditions, red flags, or life-threatening symptoms requiring hospital evaluation (e.g., chest
      pain, severe dyspnea, or altered mental status).
    • Patients meeting the below criteria, who should be recommended transfer to hospital:
            • Patients lacking mental capacity to make informed decisions about treatment at scene.
            • Patients with no access to follow-up care or safe disposition (e.g., no caregiver, inability to access
              medications).
            • Patients <18 years old, or >60 years old.
            • Pregnant patients.
            • Patients where a history of events is unreliable.
            • Patients with self-inflicted injuries (suicidal).
            • Patients with significant, relevant co-morbidities.
            • Patients who have already called an ambulance for the same condition within the last 30 days.
            • Patients who are not registered with a general practitioner or primary heath care facility, or do not have
              readily available access to a primary health care facility.

Key Patient Assessment Points
    1. Clinical Stability Confirmation
           • Ensure robust secondary assessment and patient history is completed.
           • If applicable, verify the resolution of presenting symptoms after interventions (e.g., normalized blood
             glucose).
           • Confirm absence of red flags (e.g., persistent pain, abnormal neurological findings, or unstable vital signs).
           • Ensure patient is alert, oriented, and free from conditions requiring further diagnostic evaluation (e.g.,
             infection).
    2. Mental Capacity Assessment
           • Evaluate patient’s ability to understand their condition, treatment provided, and risks of remaining at scene.
           • Assess factors which may impair capacity (e.g., intoxication, confusion, or language barriers).
    3. Follow-Up Care Feasibility
           • Confirm access to follow-up care (e.g., primary care physician, clinic, or pharmacy for medications).
           • Verify presence of a responsible caregiver or safe environment if patient remains at scene.
    4. Patient Education Needs
           • Identify need for education on condition management, warning signs, and when to seek further medical
             care.

Patient Management & Interventions
   1. Primary Survey
          • Ensure airway patency, adequate breathing, and circulation as per Universal Care CPG.
          • Address immediate life-threatening conditions before considering treatment at scene.
   2. Secondary Survey
          • Conduct a focused examination as per Universal Care CPG to confirm resolution of symptoms and absence
            of red flags.
   3. Consider Red Flags
          • Persistent or worsening symptoms despite intervention (e.g., unresolved hypoglycemia, ongoing allergic
            reaction).
          • Abnormal vital signs which may indicate instability.
          • Altered mental status, intoxication, or inability to make informed decisions.
          • Lack of follow-up care access or unsafe disposition environment (e.g., no caregiver, homelessness).
          • Suspected underlying conditions requiring hospital evaluation (e.g., infection, occult injury).
   4. Treatment Plan and Risks
          • Explain treatment plan to the patient and carer.
          • Explain risks and benefits of treatment and/or transfer.
          • If patient desires to go to the hospital, transport patient to the nearest hospital.
   5. Vitals Signs Reassessment Post Medication or Interventions
        • Post-medication or intervention, reassess and document all vital signs, to confirm patient stability.


Patient Safety Considerations
   • Patient Decision
          • If the patient insists on going to the hospital, transport patient to hospital.
   • Mental Capacity
          • Ensure patient or caregiver understands risks of remaining at scene; if capacity is impaired, transport the
             patient to hospital.
           • Use interpreters or translation tools for language barriers to confirm understanding.


Clinical Insights & Additional Guidance
   • Patient Safety First: Ensure that the decision not to transport is clinically justified, with no immediate threat to life or
     need for further diagnostics/interventions.
   • Clear Documentation: Record full clinical assessment, including vital signs, history, examination findings, and
     rationale for non-transport.
   • Capacity & Consent: Confirm that the patient has decision-making capacity and has consented to remain at scene.
     For minors or vulnerable populations, ensure proper guardian involvement.
   • Appropriate Safety Netting: Provide clear instructions to the patient/family regarding red flags, expected symptoms,
     and when to seek further care.
   • Repeat Access: If the patient has called EMS more than once for the same complaint within 24 hours, transport
     should strongly be considered.
   • Cultural and Social Considerations: Always assess for underlying social factors, including neglect, abuse, or poor
     living conditions, which may require transport or escalation.

Documentation
   • Complete a comprehensive electronic Patient Care Record (ePCR), capturing the following elements to support
     continuity of care. Including:
           • Identification: Patient’s name, age, gender.
           • Mechanism / Medical Complaint: Description of the minor complaint or resolved symptoms (e.g., mild
             allergic reaction, minor laceration).

          • Injuries / Information: History of presenting problem, treatment provided, patient’s past medical history
            relevant to the event.
          • Signs: Vital signs (including pre- and post-treatment), AVPU, GCS if indicated, resolution of abnormal signs.

Key Performance Indicators
   1. Ensure all patients treated at scene have stable vital signs documented in the patient care report.
