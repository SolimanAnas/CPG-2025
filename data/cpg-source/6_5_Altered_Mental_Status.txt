CPG 6.5           Altered Mental Status

Patient Care Goals
   1. Identify and manage life-threatening causes of altered mental status.
   2. Stabilize the patient’s condition through appropriate interventions to restore normal mental status or prevent
      further deterioration.

Patient Inclusion & Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion Criteria
    • Patients presenting with altered mental status, including:
          • Confusion, disorientation, or impaired responsiveness.
          • Lethargy, agitation, or unresponsiveness.
          • Inability to follow commands or respond appropriately to stimuli.
          • Symptoms ranging from mild confusion to coma.
    • Suspected underlying causes such as hypoglycemia, hypoxia, stroke, overdose, infection, or trauma.
Exclusion Criteria
    • None noted.


Key Patient Assessment Points
   1. Neurological Status Assessment
       • Evaluate level of consciousness using AVPU (Alert, Verbal, Painful, Unresponsive) or Glasgow Coma Scale
          (GCS).
       • Assess for focal neurological deficits (e.g., unequal pupils, motor weakness, or speech impairment).
       • Note behavioral changes (e.g., agitation, combativeness, or lethargy).
   2. History
       • Identify potential causes through SAMPLE history:
                • Recent events (e.g., trauma, drug use, or medication changes).
                • Medical history (e.g., diabetes, epilepsy, stroke, or psychiatric disorders).
                • Medication use (e.g., insulin, sedatives, or missed doses).
       • Check for medical alert bracelets or documentation indicating conditions like diabetes or epilepsy.
   3. Differential Diagnosis
       • Assess for possible causes using the AEIOD TIPS pneumonic:
                  • A – Alcohol, Abuse, Atypical migraine
                  • E – Epilepsy, Electrolytes
                  • I – Insulin (hypoglycemia)
                  • O – Oxygen, Overdose
                  • U – Uremia (kidney failure)
                  • T – Trauma, Tumor
                  • I – Infection
                  • P – Psych, Poisoning
                  • S – Seizure, Subarachnoid hemorrhage, Sepsis
   4. Glucose and Oxygen Assessment
       • Check random blood sugar (RBS) to identify hypoglycemia (<70 mg/dL) or hyperglycemia (>200 mg/dL).
       • Assess for hypoxia via pulse oximetry (SpO2 <94%).
   5. Environmental and Toxicological Clues
       • Look for evidence of drug or alcohol use (e.g., pill bottles, drug paraphernalia).
       • Assess for environmental exposures (e.g., carbon monoxide, chemicals).
   6. Determine Triage Category
       • Determine Triage Category as per Patient Triage Categories CPG.
       • Red Priority:
            • GCS < or equal to 9, or progressive deterioration in GCS
            • Unresponsive or responsive only to pain

CPG 6.5           Altered Mental Status

Patient Management & Interventions
   1. Primary and Secondary Survey
        • Perform primary and secondary survey as per Universal Care CPG.
   2. Consider Red Flags
        • Unresponsiveness or coma (AVPU).
        • Hypoglycemia (RBS <70 mg/dL) go to Hypoglycemia CPG.
        • Severe hyperglycemia (RBS >400 mg/dL) go to Hyperglycemia CPG.
        • Hypoxia (SpO2 <90%) or respiratory distress.
        • Suspected stroke (e.g., focal neurological deficits, sudden onset) go to Stroke CPG.
        • Signs of overdose (e.g., pinpoint pupils, respiratory depression) go to Overdose CPG.
   3. Consider ALS back-up
        • Consider the requirement for ALS back-up as per Universal Care CPG.
   4. Evaluate for Signs of Shock or Instability
        • Hypotension (SBP <90 mmHg in adults, or age-specific for pediatrics), tachycardia, or poor perfusion (e.g.,
          cool, clammy skin, delayed capillary refill >2 seconds).
        • If shock is present, initiate fluid resuscitation and address underlying causes.
        • Manage Shock as per appropriate Shock Guideline.
   5. Establish IV Access
        • Indicated for hypotension, suspected hypoglycemia, or anticipated medication administration.
        • Consider intraosseous (IO) access if IV access cannot be obtained
   6. Patient Reassessment
        • Frequently Reassess the patient as per Universal Care CPG.
        • Specific Patient Reassessment Points:
             • Neurological Status
                • Reassess AVPU or GCS frequently to monitor for deterioration or improvement.


Patient Safety Considerations
   • It is difficult to accurately assess patients with an altered mental status. All patients with an altered mental status
     should be transported to hospital.

Clinical Insights & Other Guidance
   • None noted


Documentation
   • Identification: Full patient name, age, and gender.
   • Mechanism / Medical Complaint: Nature and progression of altered mental status, suspected underlying cause
     (e.g., hypoglycemia, overdose, stroke).
   • Injuries / Information: Clues to contributing causes (e.g., trauma, alcohol, drug paraphernalia), presence of alert
     bracelets.
   • Signs: GCS or AVPU scale, pupil size/reactivity, vital signs (BP, HR, SpO₂, temperature), RBS results.
   • Treatment:
         • Glucose administration: route, dose, and timing.
         • Oxygen therapy and SpO₂ levels.
         • IV fluids or medications if given.
         • Response to treatment and changes in mental status.
   • Allergies: Any known allergies, especially medications.
   • Medications: Recent or chronic medications (e.g., insulin, sedatives, psychiatric drugs).
   • Background: Known conditions such as diabetes, epilepsy, stroke, psychiatric illness.
   • Other:
         • Environmental or toxicological clues.
         • Scene information and bystander reports.
