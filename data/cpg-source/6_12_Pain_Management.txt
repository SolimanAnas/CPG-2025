CPG 6.12            Pain Management

Patient Care Goals
    1. Effectively manage acute pain to improve patient comfort and reduce distress.
    2. Administer appropriate analgesics based on pain severity and underlying cause while minimizing adverse effects.

Patient Inclusion & Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion Criteria
    • Patients presenting with acute pain of any origin (e.g., medical, traumatic, or unspecified), including:
          • Pain reported on a 0-10 scale (mild: 1-3, moderate: 4-6, severe: 7-10).
          • Pain associated with conditions such as chest pain, abdominal pain, burns, fractures, or sickle cell crisis.
          • Patients requiring pain relief to facilitate transport.
    • Verbal or non-verbal indicators of pain (e.g., grimacing, guarding, or crying).
Exclusion Criteria
    • Patients with no reported or observed pain.
    • Pain clearly attributable to a chronic condition requiring specific non-prehospital management (e.g., chronic
      neuropathic pain without acute exacerbation).

Key Patient Assessment Points
    1. Pain Assessment
         • Evaluate Pain Characteristics using OPQRST:
                  • Onset, Provocation, Quality, Radiation, Severity, Timing.
         • Evaluate Pain Severity:
                  • In Adult Patients and Pediatric Patients > 3 years:
                      • Patients who can verbalize: Use a 0-10 pain scale.
                      • Patient who cannot verbalize: Use the Wong-Baker FACES scale.
                  • In Pediatric patients <3 years:
                      • Use the FLACC pain scale.
         • Reassess pain score after interventions to evaluate response.
    2. Pain Source Identification
         • Identify the underlying cause or trigger of pain (e.g., cardiac, musculoskeletal, visceral, or sickle cell crisis).
         • Note associated symptoms (e.g., nausea, shortness of breath, or fever) to guide treatment.
    3. Contraindications to Analgesics
         • Assess for allergies to analgesics (e.g., morphine, paracetamol).
         • Identify conditions that may contraindicate specific medications (e.g., hypotension for opioids, liver disease
           for paracetamol).
    4. Functional Impact of Pain
         • Evaluate how pain affects patient mobility, cooperation, or clinical stability (e.g., inability to lie flat for transfer
           due to pain).
    5. Determine Triage Category
         • Determine Triage Category as per Patient Triage Categories CPG.
                • Note that pain severity does not influence Triage Category. However, severe pain is an indicator for a
                  significant underlying issue.

Patient Management & Interventions
    1. Consider ALS back-up
         • Consider the requirement for ALS back-up as per Universal Care CPG.
         • ALS back-up should be requested for Severe pain (7-10/10).
    2. Consider Red Flags
         • Pain associated with life-threatening conditions (e.g., chest pain suggestive of myocardial infarction,
           abdominal pain with shock).
         • Hypotension (SBP <90 mmHg in adults, or age-specific for pediatrics) or respiratory depression.
    3. Evaluate for Signs of Shock or Instability

        • Hypotension, tachycardia, or poor perfusion (e.g., cool, clammy skin, delayed capillary refill >2 seconds).
        • If shock is present, prioritize treatment of the underlying cause and use caution with analgesics (e.g., opioids
         in hypotensive patients).
   4. Consider Non-Pharmacological Interventions
       • Positioning: Position the patient for comfort (e.g., semi-Fowler’s for chest pain, splinting for fractures).
       • Reassurance: Use calm communication to reduce anxiety and perceived pain.
       • Cooling/Heat: Apply cold packs for acute injuries or heat for muscle spasms, as appropriate.
   5. Consider Establish IV Access
       • Indicated for moderate to severe pain requiring IV analgesics or for patients at risk of deterioration.
       • Consider intraosseous (IO) access if IV access cannot be obtained
   6. Pharmacological Interventions
       • Mild Pain (0-3/10)
               • Paracetamol PO
                   • Adults: 1 g PO
                   • Pediatrics:
                         • PO 15 mg/kg (max 1 g) (if available)
                         • PR <5years: 125mg
                         • PR >5years: 250mg
       • Moderate Pain (4-6/10)
               • Paracetamol IV
                   • Adults: 1g
                   • Pediatrics: 15mg/kg (if micro-drip set available)
                     • IV paracetamol is not recommended for children less than 33kg.
                     • IV paracetamol generally should not be given to pediatrics unless a micro-drip administration
                       set is available.
               • Diclofenac
                   • Adults: 50-75 mg IM.
                   • Pediatrics: Not recommended.
               • Nefopam
                   • Adults: 20 mg IV, diluted in 100ml NaCl administered over 10 minutes.
                   • Pediatrics: Not recommended.
       • Moderate Pain with Associated with Painful Procedures
               • Medications as per moderate pain, and/
               • Methoxyflurane (Penthrox)
                   • Adults: 3ml Inhaled dose
                   • Pediatrics: 3ml Inhaled dose
       • Severe Pain (7-10/10)
               • Medications as per moderate pain, and/
               • Methoxyflurane (Penthrox)
                   • Adults: 3ml Inhaled dose
                   • Pediatrics: 3ml Inhaled dose
               • Morphine
                   • Adults:
                         • IV: 2.5 mg increments, titrated every 5-10 minutes (max 20 mg).
                         • IM 5-10mg.
                   • Pediatrics:
                         • IV: 0.1mg/kg (max 5 mg), titrated every 5-10 minutes (max 20 mg).
                         • IM: 0.1mg/kg (max 10 mg).
               • Pethidine
                   • Adults:
                         • IV: 25-50mg. Single dose only.

                          • IM: 50-100mg. Single dose only.
                     • Pediatrics: 1 mg/kg IV/IM.
   7. Patient Reassessment
       • Frequently Reassess the patient as per Universal Care CPG.
       • Specific Patient Reassessment Points:
              • Monitor Pain and Response
                 • Reassess pain score every 5-15 minutes after interventions to evaluate efficacy.
                 • Monitor for adverse effects (e.g., respiratory depression or hypotension).


Patient Safety Considerations
   • ALS back up should be considered for patients with a pain score greater than 7.
   • All patients who receive narcotic analgesia must be transported to the hospital for further evaluation and
     monitoring, regardless of symptom improvement at the scene

Clinical Insights & Additional Guidance
   • Pain Management Strategy
         • Aim for adequate relief of pain that is satisfactory to the patient or best achievable under the circumstances.
         • Adopt a multimodal approach to pain management, combining multiple medications in addition to non-
           pharmacological interventions to enhance relief.
         • This strategy reduces reliance on any single medication and minimize side effects associated with high doses.
         • If pain relief is inadequate, proceed to the next step on the pain management ladder.
   • Clinical Judgment
         • Decisions to give analgesia must be based on clinical assessment and not directly on a linear scale
         • Practitioners may make a clinical judgment and commence pain relief on a higher level of the pain ladder.
         • Base decisions to administer analgesia on clinical assessment, patient’s overall condition, vital signs, and not
           solely on a linear pain scale.
         • Consider:
              • Patient’s age, weight, and developmental stage.
              • Known allergies or contraindications.
              • Potential side effects and interactions, especially considering pediatric physiology.
   • Procedural Pain
         • This is pain associated with painful procedures such as splinting or during movement over rough terrain.
           During these short times, pain can be severe, but the underlying pain is moderate.
         • It is encouraged to administer Penthrox for moderate pain in this circumstance.
   • Methoxyflurane (Penthrox)
         • Provided adequate instruction and coaching is given Pentrhox can be an excellent pain reliever.
         • Penthrox is generally short acting – additional analgesia (such as IV paracetamol) should be administered in
           addition with Penthrox.
   • Prophylactic Antiemetics
         • Routine administration of metoclopramide prophylactically for nausea and vomiting associated with morphine
           is not recommended.

Documentation
   • Identification: Full patient name, age, and gender.
   • Mechanism / Medical Complaint: Description of pain onset, severity, duration, and associated symptoms.
   • Injuries / Information: Underlying cause or condition (e.g., fracture, sickle cell crisis), known diagnoses, and medical
     history.
   • Signs: Pain score (pre and post treatment), vitals, AVPU/GCS, physical findings such as guarding or swelling.
   • Treatment:
         • Route and dose of analgesic(s) administered.
         • Response to analgesics and reassessment of pain score.
   • Allergies: Known allergies to medications like morphine, paracetamol, or NSAIDs.
   • Medications: Current medications that may interact with analgesics.
   • Background: Relevant chronic illnesses or contraindications (e.g., renal/liver disease).
   • Other: ECG if indicated.



Key Performance Indicators
 1. Document the pain score for all patients before and after administering analgesia.
 2. Administer appropriate analgesia to patients with a pain score >7 to reduce pain score by at least 3 points.
