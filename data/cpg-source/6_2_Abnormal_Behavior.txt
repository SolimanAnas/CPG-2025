CPG 6.2           Abnormal Behavior

Patient Care Goals
   1. Adequate assessment and treatment of agitated, violent or uncooperative patients.
   2. Maximizing and maintaining safety for the patient, EMS personnel, and others.
   3. Ensure appropriate pharmacological management.

Patient Inclusion & Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion Criteria
    • Patients presenting with abnormal or unusual behavior who are a danger to themselves or others.
Exclusion Criteria
    • Patients with abnormal behavior secondary to medical conditions such as traumatic brain injury or hypoxia.


Key Patient Assessment Points
   1. Secondary Survey
        • Once patient compliance has been gained, a patient history and/or physical examination should be
          undertaken to exclude possible reversible causes as per the Universal Care CPG.
        • This assessment should include a complete set of vital signs as a minimum.
   2. Investigation of Alternative Causes
        • Abnormal behavior may be due to a cause other than psychiatric illness. Alternative causes (such as
          hypoglycemia) should be assessed.
   3. Sedation Assessment Tool
        • The sedation assessment tool should be used to guide patient management and communication between
          healthcare providers (for example with Tele-EMS).
        • Application of the tool is observational only and does not require patient cooperation or participation.
        • Sedation Assessment Tool: Assessment Table3

        Score      Responsiveness                                         Speech

          +3       Combative, violent, out of control                     Continual loud outbursts

          +2       Very anxious and agitated                              Loud outbursts

          +1       Anxious/restless                                       Normal/talkative

          0        Awake and calm/cooperative                             Speaks normally

          -1       Asleep but rouses if name is called                    Slurring or prominent slowing

          -2       Responds to physical stimulation                       Few recognizable words

          -3       No response to stimulation                             Nil

   4. Determine Triage Category
       • Determine Triage Category as per Patient Triage Categories CPG.

Patient Management & Interventions
   1. Primary and Secondary Survey
        • Perform primary and secondary survey as per the Universal Care CPG.
        • It may be difficult or unsafe to complete adequate primary or secondary survey’s initially in the agitated
          patient, this may need to be delayed until patient compliance has been gained.

   2. Attempt verbal de-escalation
        • Verbal de-escalation is under-utilized.
        • Attempt verbal de-escalation by:
               • Allowing sufficient time to attempt verbal de-escalation
               • Having only one person speaking with the patient.
               • Introduce yourself; state that you are here to help and provide reassurance.
               • Use short sentences and keep messages simple. Repetition is essential as the patient will usually have a
                  short attention span.
               • Limit unnecessary noise and disturbances (radios, lights, sirens, strong vehicle movements)
               • Try to establish a rapport with the patient
   2. Request Police Assistance
        • If there is a risk of harm to the patient, EMS staff or bystanders.
        • If restraint is anticipated to be required
   3. Consider ALS back-up if sedation is required
        • Consider contacting Tele-EMS prior to requesting ALS back-up if the requirement for sedation is unclear.
   4. Sedation/Pharmacological Management
        • Sedation Objectives:
                  • Sedation is not required in patients who are compliant and do not demonstrate a risk to safety.
                  • The objectives of sedation are to gain patient compliance and facilitate assessment and safe transfer
                    of the patient to an appropriate facility.
                  • For most patients, the objective of sedation is to achieve a Sedation Assessment Tool Score of 0 to -1.
        • Medication Selection:
                  • When administering sedation, agitated or violent patients pose a safety risk to ambulance staff
                    (primarily through direct physical violence, or needle stick injury).
                  • When considering sedation agents, the first medication selected should be targeted at gaining control
                    of the patient/achieving patient compliance with a single intervention.
                  • For further medication selection guidance, see the Sedation Assessment Score Based Medication
                    Guideline below.
   5. Post-Sedation Monitoring
        • If/when the patient is compliant, an assessment should be undertaken to identify any underlying cause. This
          includes a full set of vital signs.
        • Ongoing Pulse oximetry, end-tidal co2 (via nasal cannula ETCO2) and 4-lead ECG monitoring is mandatory for
          all patients that have received sedation.
        • Oxygen (via nasal cannula as a minimum), should be considered for all sedated patients with a Sedation
          Assessment Tool Score of -1 or less.
        • IV access should be obtained as soon as possible after the patient has become compliant.

CPG 6.2           Abnormal Behavior

Patient Safety Considerations
   • Sedation
        • Avoid pharmaceutical interventions for sedation, unless is it required for patient or practitioner safety.
        • All patients who have received sedation must be transferred to hospital.
        • The medical staff administering sedation must remain at the patient’s side at all times, ensuring continuous
           monitoring throughout the procedure.
   • Patient Positioning
         • Sedated patients with altered mental status, should be placed in the recovery position.
   • Positioning of agitated patients
         • Placing the stretcher in a sitting position prevents aspiration and reduces the patient’s physical strength by
           placing the abdominal muscles in the flexed position.
         • Do not put the patient in a prone position to ensure patient safety.


Key Considerations & Other Guidance
   • Ensure scene safety before engaging with the patient; involve police if there is potential for violence or risk.
   • All sedatives carry the risk of adverse effects namely, respiratory depression, apnea and hypotension.
   • Patients who have been administered multiple doses or multiple medications have an increased risk of adverse
     events and may require airway management.
   • Police Restraint Use:
         • Work with police to reposition restrained patients to ensure airway, breathing and circulation are not impaired.



Documentation
   • Identification: Full patient name, age, and gender.
   • Mechanism / Medical Complaint: Abnormal behavior, agitation, aggression, or psychiatric history.
   • Injuries / Information: Any physical injuries, psychiatric conditions, substance use, or medications.
   • Signs: GCS or AVPU, vital signs, signs of trauma, blood glucose level.
   • Treatment:
       • Verbal de-escalation methods used.
       • Sedation: drug, dose, route, response.
       • Physical restraint: indication, duration, staff involved.
   • Allergies: Known medication or substance allergies.
   • Medications: Current psychiatric medications or recent changes.
   • Background: History of mental illness, recent events, prior admissions in psychiatric ward (e.g., loss, trauma).
   • Other: Police involvement, caregiver input, capacity assessment.


Key Performance Indicators
   1. When sedation is administered, ensure documentation of the medication name, dose and effect.
