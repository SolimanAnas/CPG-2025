CPG 2.4           COPD

Patient Care Goals
   • Perform respiratory assessment to identify severity.
   • Assure adequate oxygenation and ventilation.
   • Recognize impending respiratory failure.
   • Deliver appropriate therapy by differentiating likely cause of respiratory distress.
   • Alleviate respiratory distress.


Patient Inclusion & Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion Criteria
    • Patients with a history of COPD presenting with features of exacerbation, including; increase in cough, sputum, or
      dyspnea beyond normal day-to-day variation; worsening hypoxemia; tachycardia; hypertension; cyanosis; altered
      mental status; hypercapnia; accessory respiratory muscle use; pursed-lip exhalation.
Exclusion Criteria
    • Patients presenting with difficulty of breathing from another cause e.g. asthma.


Key Patient Assessment Points
   1. Primary & Secondary Survey
          • Perform primary and secondary survey as per Universal Care CPG.
          • Identify Features of COPD Exacerbation:
              • Increase in cough, sputum, or dyspnea beyond normal day-to-day variation.
              • Worsening hypoxemia.
              • Tachycardia.
              • Hypertension.
              • Cyanosis.
              • Altered mental status.
              • Hypercapnia.
              • Accessory respiratory muscle use.
              • Pursed-lip exhalation.
          • Determine history of COPD.
              • Onset of symptoms.
              • Concurrent symptoms (e.g., fever, cough, rhinorrhea, tongue/lip swelling, rash, labored breathing,
                foreign body aspiration).
              • Usual triggers of symptoms (e.g., cigarette smoke, change in weather, upper respiratory infections,
                exercise).
              • Treatments prior to EMS arrival: Oxygen, inhaler, nebulizer, other treatments, chronic or recent steroids.
              • Hospitalizations: Number of emergency department visits in the past year, number of hospital
                admissions in the past year, number of ICU admissions (ever), previously intubated (ever).
   2. Determine Triage Category
          • Determine Triage Category as per Patient Triage Categories CPG.


Patient Management & Interventions
   1. Consider ALS back-up
           • Consider the requirement for ALS back-up as per Universal Care CPG.
   2. Administer Oxygen
           • Start with nasal oxygen at 2 L/min and increase p.r.n. up to a maximum flow rate of 6 L/min for nasal
             cannula.
           • If no improvement is observed after reaching the maximum, change to a non-rebreather mask (NRB) at 10-
             15 L/min to maintain SpO2 at 88-92% to avoid hyperoxia.
   3. Administer Nebulized Salbutamol
           • Administer nebulized Salbutamol 5mg Neb (1st choice), repeatable as required every 5 minutes.
   4. If symptoms do not resolve, or severe respiratory distress:

          • Administer nebulized Combivent
               • 0.5mg Ipratropium Bromide + 3mg Salbutamol, nebulized, single dose.
          • Administer Hydrocortisone
               • Hydrocortisone: 100-200mg IV. Slow IV over 2 minutes, or diluted in 100mL NaCl administered over 2-
                    5 minutes. Or/
                  • Hydrocortisone: 100mg IM.
   5. Patient Reassessment
           • Reassess for severe distress or worsening symptoms.
   6. If continued worsening symptoms, or severe respiratory distress:
           • Consider consulting Tele-EMS for complicated cases or for guidance on CPAP/BiPAP settings application
           • If nebulizer treatments are ineffective.
                • Consider CPAP
           • If inspiratory support is required.
                • Consider CPAP/BiPAP
   7. Patient Reassessment
           • Frequently Reassess the patient as per Universal Care CPG.


Patient Safety Considerations
   • Risk of hypercapnic respiratory failure:
         • Patients with moderate to severe COPD are at risk; titrate oxygen to maintain SpO2 88-92% to prevent
           respiratory acidosis, organ dysfunction, or coma.
   • Consider other causes (e.g., pulmonary embolism, pneumonia, ARDS, pulmonary edema) if adequate oxygenation
     cannot be achieved with low FiO2.
   • COPD triggers: Consider potential triggers (e.g., viral/bacterial infections, environmental pollution) and review
     patient history, even in individuals without a known COPD diagnosis, to guide management.

Clinical Insights & Additional Information
   • Hydrocortisone is preferred to be administered via the IV route when possible.


Documentation
   • Identification: Full patient name, age, and gender.
   • Mechanism / Medical Complaint: Presenting symptoms (e.g., increased cough, dyspnea), history of COPD.
   • Injuries / Information: Relevant history (e.g., triggers, comorbidities).
   • Signs:
        • Full vital signs including pulse, respiratory rate, SpO2, EtCO2.
        • Two sets of vital signs unless critical patient condition precludes.
        • Exacerbation features (e.g., cyanosis, accessory muscle use).
   • Treatment:
        • Oxygen administration and nebulized medication doses.
        • All interventions: Salbutamol, Combivent, Hydrocortisone, CPAP/BiPAP, and response.
        • Justification if interventions were withheld.
   • Allergies: Documented allergies and drug interactions.
   • Medications: All pre-existing medications and any administered in the field.
   • Background: Relevant medical history, comorbidities, and recent procedures.
   • Other:
        • Communication with Telemedicine (time and instructions).
        • Response to interventions.
        • Consideration of reversible causes and actions taken.
