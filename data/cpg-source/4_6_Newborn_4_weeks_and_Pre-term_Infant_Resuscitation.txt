CPG 4.6           Newborn and Pre-term Infant Resuscitation

Patient Care Goals
   1. Provide routine care to the newborn infant.
   2. Rapidly identify newly born infants requiring resuscitative efforts.
   3. Establish spontaneous breathing and a stable heart rate in newborns and preterm infants.
   4. Maintain normothermia and provide adequate oxygenation while minimizing complications such as hyperoxia or
      hypoxia.
   5. High-quality chest compressions/CPR with minimal interruptions from recognition of cardiac arrest until
      confirmation of ROSC or termination of care.

Patient Inclusion & Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion Criteria
    • Newborns (< 4 weeks) and preterm infants requiring resuscitation due to:
          • Apnea or gasping respiration.
          • Heart rate <100 bpm at 1 minute post-delivery.
          • Cyanosis or pale appearance.
          • Poor muscle tone or lack of crying.
Exclusion Criteria
    • Infants >4 weeks of age (See Pediatric Resuscitation CPG).


Key Patient Assessment Points
   1. Assessment
       • Patient History
         • Date and time of birth
         • Prenatal history (prenatal care, substance abuse, multiple gestation, maternal illness.
         • Birth history (maternal fever, presence of meconium, maternal bleeding, difficult delivery – e.g. shoulder
           dystocia, prolapsed cord or nuchal core, breech)
         • Estimated gestational age (may be based on last menstrual period)
   2. Physical Examination & Assessment
       • Respiratory rate and effort (strong, weak, or absent; regular or irregular)
       • Signs of respiratory distress (grunting, nasal flaring, retractions, gasping, apnea)
       • Pulse oximetry should be considered if resuscitative efforts are initiated or if supplemental oxygen is
         administered.
       • Heart rate (fast, slow, or absent)
           • Precordium, umbilical stump, or brachial pulse may be used.
           • Auscultation is preferred since palpation of the umbilical stump is less accurate.
       • Muscle tone (poor or strong)
       • Color/Appearance (central cyanosis, acrocyanosis, pallor, normal)
       • APGAR score at 1 minute and 5-minute post full delivery of infant.
       • APGAR Score Table:

         • Determine Triage Category as per Patient Triage Categories CPG.

Patient Management & Interventions
   1. Consider ALS back-up
         • Consider the requirement for ALS back-up as per Universal Care CPG.
   2. Immediately After Delivery
         • Assess for tone, breathing or crying.
              • If tone, breathing or crying is adequate, no resuscitation is necessary.
                   • Go to Childbirth CPG.
         • If weak cry, signs of respiratory distress, poor tone, or preterm gestation:
              • Position airway (sniffing position) and clear airway as needed.
              • If signs of respiratory distress with airway obstruction, suction mouth then nose; routine suctioning is not
                recommended.
              • Dry, warm, and stimulate
                     • Wrap infant in dry towel/thermal blanket or clean plastic bag up to the neck, to keep infant as warm
                       as possible; keep head covered if possible.
   3. Reassess Breathing and Heart Rate
         • If Laboured Breathing or Persistent Cyanosis:
              • Attach Sp02 monitor
              • Consider Supplementary 02 (start with low-Fi02 initially)
         • Apnea or Gasping:
              • Position Airway
                   • Consider Airway Adjuncts (OPA/NPA/Intubation)
              • Assist Ventilation (PPV) at 40-60 bpm.
                   • Do not add oxygen initially.
              • Attach Sp02 monitor
              • Attach ECG Monitor
         • Consider Cutting Cord:
              • If the patient does not rapidly improve with interventions, or requires immediate resuscitation:
              • Clamp the cord in three places (10cm, 15cm, 20cm) and cut between the 2 clamps furthest away from
                the newborn.
   4. If Heart Rate < 100
         • Initiate BVM ventilation with room air at 40-60 breaths per minute for 90 seconds with room air.
                • The primary indicator of effective ventilation is improvement in heart rate.
                • Rates and volumes of ventilation can be variable, only use the minimum necessary rate and volume to
                   achieve chest rise and a change in heart rate.
         • Evaluate heart rate every 30 seconds
                • If no improvement after 90 seconds, change oxygen delivery to 100% Fi02 until the heart rate normalizes
   4. If Heart Rate < 60
         • Initiate chest compressions:
                • Two-thumb-encircling-hands technique is preferred.
                • 3:1 chest compression – ventilation ratio.
         • Consider endotracheal intubation or supraglottic airway.
         • Establish IV or IO Access.
         • Administer Epinephrine:
                • Adrenaline: 0.01 mg/kg IV/IO every 3-5 minutes if the heart rate remains less than 60bpm.
                • IV Fluid Administration
         • Administer 20 mL/kg normal saline IV/IO for signs of shock or post-resuscitative care.

Patient Safety Considerations
   • Hypothermia is common in newborns and worsens outcomes of nearly all post-natal complications.
        • Ensure heat retention by drying the infant thoroughly, covering the head, and wrapping the baby in dry cloth.
        • Kangaroo care advisable where no resuscitative care is needed is an effective warming technique (i.e. placing
           the infant skin-to-skin directly against the mother’s chest and wrapping them together)
         • Newborn infants are prone to hypothermia which may lead to hypoglycemia, hypoxia, and lethargy. Aggressive
           warming techniques should be initiated, including drying, swaddling, and warm blankets covering body and
           head.
   • Preterm infants (<23 weeks) may receive comfort care based on parental wishes; resuscitation is not routinely
     recommended.

Clinical Insights & Additional Guidance
   • Most newborns require only drying, warming, and stimulating to help them transition from fetal respiration to
    newborn respiration.
   • Resuscitation Strategy
        • The resuscitation sequence can be remembered as: Dry, Warm, and Stimulate – Ventilate – Evaluate – and
          Resuscitate.
   • Placental Delivery
          • If the placenta is delivered, place it in a biohazard bag and transport it with the patient to the hospital.
   • Newborn Pulse Oximetry:
       • Measuring the pulse oximetry on the right hand provides the most accurate oxygen saturation in infants that are
        transitioning from fetal to normal circulation.
      • At 60 seconds, 60% is the target with an increase of 5% every minute until 5 minutes of life when pulse oximetry
        is 80-85%.
      • Projected Pulse Oximetry in Infants Over Time:
                                                                Projected Increase in Pulse
                                 Time Since Birth
                                                                    Oximeter Over Time
                                      1 minute                              60-65%
                                     2 minutes                              65-70%
                                     3 minutes                              70-75%
                                     4 minutes                              75-80%
                                     5 minutes                              80-85%
                                    10 minutes                              85-90%

   • Hypoxia & Oxygen Administration
        • Both hypoxia and excess oxygen administration can result in harm to the infant. If prolonged oxygen use is
          required, titrate to maintain an Sp02 of 85%-95%
        • Increase in heart rate is the most reliable indicator of effective resuscitative efforts.
   • Pertinent Assessment Findings
      • If there is any doubt in gestational age in the field, resuscitation efforts should be initiated.
      • Acrocyanosis, a blue discoloration of the distal extremities, is a common finding in the newly born infant
         transitioning to extrauterine life. This must be differentiated from central cyanosis.
   • Pre-Term Infant Resuscitation
         • Avoid routine resuscitation <22 weeks unless parental wishes and clinical judgement indicate otherwise.

Documentation
   • Identification: Infant’s gender, estimated gestational age, and time of birth.
   • Mechanism / Medical Complaint: Symptoms such as apnea, gasping, poor muscle tone, cyanosis.
   • Injuries / Information:
        • Prenatal history (e.g., prenatal care, maternal substance abuse, multiple gestation, maternal illness).
        • Birth history (e.g., maternal fever, meconium presence, maternal bleeding, difficult delivery such as shoulder
            dystocia, prolapsed cord, nuchal cord, or breech).
   • Signs:
         • Vital signs (heart rate via precordium/umbilical stump/brachial pulse, respiratory rate, SpO₂ if monitored).
         • APGAR score at 1 and 5 minutes post-delivery (appearance, pulse, grimace, activity, respirations).
         • Physical findings (e.g., central cyanosis, acrocyanosis, pallor, grunting, nasal flaring, retractions, muscle
            tone).
         • Blood glucose level (if checked).
   • Treatment:
         • Airway management (e.g., positioning in sniffing position, suctioning mouth then nose if obstruction present).
         • Warming and stimulation (e.g., drying, wrapping in thermal blanket/plastic bag, covering head).
         • Ventilation details
         • Airway adjuncts (e.g., OPA/NPA, endotracheal intubation, supraglottic airway).
         • IV/IO access placement and fluid administration
         • Medication administration
         • Cord management
         • Oxygen therapy
   • Allergies: Not typically applicable for newborns.
   • Medications: Note any maternal medications (e.g., tocolytics) relevant to the infant’s condition.
   • Background: Maternal chronic illnesses (e.g., diabetes, hypertension), recent hospitalization, or obstetric
     complications.
   • Other:
         • Placenta management (e.g., placed in biohazard bag for transport if delivered).
         • Dispatch and hospital coordination notes (e.g., notification of neonatal resuscitation and receiving facility
            preparation).
         • Note parental wishes and clinical judgment for preterm infants <23 weeks.


Key Performance Indicators
   1. Provide chest compressions and ventilation within 30 seconds of the heart rate falling below 60bpm.
