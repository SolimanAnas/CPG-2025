Patient Care Goals
  1. Rapidly identify tachycardia in infants and children and determine whether it is compromising perfusion or mental
     status.
  2. Distinguish between physiologic sinus tachycardia (often a response to stress, pain or fever) and dangerous
     tachyarrhythmias requiring intervention.
  3. Maintain adequate oxygenation, perfusion, and cerebral function.
  4. Systematically explore and correct potential underlying causes, such as:
         • Excessive stimulants or medications (caffeine, decongestants, thyroid preparations, diet aids)
         • Illicit drug use (e.g., amphetamines, cocaine)
         • Pre-existing cardiac conditions (history of arrhythmias or structural heart disease)
         • Cardiac failure (e.g., CHF presenting as low-output tachycardia)
         • Severe infections (e.g., sepsis, pneumonia, meningitis)

Patient Inclusion & Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion Criteria
    • Infants and children ≤13 years presenting with a heart rate above normal for age, with or without signs of instability.

    • Infants and children ≤13 years presenting with a heart rate above normal for age, with signs of poor perfusion, such
      as:
            • Altered mental status.
            • Weak pulses, hypotension, prolonged capillary refill (>3 seconds).
            • Pallor, cyanosis, respiratory distress.
Exclusion Criteria
   • Age-appropriate sinus tachycardia (e.g., due to fever, pain, dehydration) without instability.
   • Known chronic arrhythmias in stable condition.

Key Patient Assessment Points
    1. Primary Survey
           • Initial Impression
                  • Use the Pediatric Assessment Triangle (PAT) to assess appearance, work of breathing, circulation.
                  • Look for evidence of shock or impending cardiac or respiratory arrest.
           • Airway & Breathing Assessment
                  • Check for signs of respiratory distress, hypoxia, or failure.
                  • Listen/Auscultate for wheezing, stridor, or silent chest.
           • Circulation Assessment
                  • Assess central and peripheral pulses for rate, regularity, and strength.
                  • Capillary refill time, skin color and temperature.
                  • Blood pressure (if feasible).
    2. Secondary Survey
           • Heart Rate & Rhythm Assessment
                  • Count HR for a full minute.
                  • Apply ECG if available to differentiate:
                        • Sinus tachycardia (gradual onset, HR appropriate for age & context).

                      • Supraventricular tachycardia (SVT): abrupt onset, very high rate (>220 infant, >180 child), narrow
                        QRS, poor perfusion.
                      • Ventricular tachycardia (VT): widened QRS.
   3. Reversible Cause Assessment
         • Assess for Reversible causes such as: fever, dehydration, pain, hypoxia, anxiety, toxins, metabolic disorders.
   4. Determine Triage Category
         • Determine Triage Category as per Patient Triage Categories CPG.
         • Pediatric tachycardia should be triaged as:
               • Red (Serious & Life-threatening):
                      • HR >220 in infants or >180 in children with signs of poor perfusion: altered mental status,
                        hypotension, weak pulses, cyanosis, or respiratory distress.
                      • Unstable tachycardia requiring immediate intervention (adenosine, cardioversion).
               • Yellow (Serious but not immediately life-threatening):
                      • HR elevated beyond normal limits but with stable perfusion, normal mental status, and no signs
                        of shock.
                      • Patient responsive to initial vagal maneuvers, stable after intervention.


Patient Management & Interventions
   1. Consider ALS back-up
        • Consider the requirement for ALS back-up as per Universal Care CPG.
        • ALS back-up should be routinely requested.
   1. Calm and systematic approach
          • Keep the child warm and minimize stress.
          • Allow caregiver presence if calming.
   2. Oxygenation & Ventilation
          • Ensure a patient airway is maintained
          • Administer Oxygen titrated to target oxygen saturation ≥94% and normocapnia (EtCO₂ 35–45 mmHg).
          • If respiratory failure or apnea, start bag-valve-mask ventilation.
   3. 12-Lead ECG
          • Perform a 12 lead ECG and continuously monitor the rhythm
   4. Measure Blood Glucose
          • Check blood glucose and, if hypoglycemia is identified, treat according to the Hypoglycemia CPG; if
            hyperglycemia is identified, refer to the Hyperglycemia CPG for management.
   5. IV Access
          • Establish IV access if indicated or in patients who:
                   • Are at risk for clinical deterioration, or
                   • Require IV fluid or medication administration.
          • Intraosseous (IO) access is indicated if IV access fails or is impractical.
   6. Cardiopulmonary Compromise Assessment
          • Stable
                   • Tachyarrhythmia with adequate perfusion (no significant hypotension, good mental status, warm
                     extremities, normal capillary refill).
          • Unstable
                   • Tachyarrhythmia with signs of poor perfusion or cardiovascular compromise, such as:
                         • Altered mental status
                         • Hypotension
                         • Weak or absent peripheral pulses
                         • Cool, mottled skin, prolonged capillary refill
                         • Respiratory distress or failure
   7. Stable Tachycardia
        • Evaluate the rhythm

                • If Narrow-Complex (likely sinus tachycardia or SVT):
                        • Assess underlying causes (such as: fever, pain, dehydration, anxiety).
                        • If sinus tachycardia: treat underlying cause.
                        • If SVT suspected:
                                 • Attempt vagal maneuvers.
                                 • If vagal maneuvers ineffective and IV/IO access available:
                                      • Administer Adenosine:

                                          • 2nd dose: 0.2 mg/kg IV/IO rapid IV push, max 12 mg.
                • If Wide-Complex:
                        • If rhythm is regular and monomorphic:
                                 • Adenosine may be administered after consultation with Tele EMS, routine

                                • Administer Adenosine:
                                      • 1st dose: 0.1 mg/kg IV/IO rapid IV push, max 6 mg.

                       • If irregular or polymorphic → consult expert; avoid adenosine.
                • Prepare for possible deterioration — have cardioversion and resuscitation equipment ready.
   8. Unstable Tachycardia (with a pulse)
       • Evaluate the Rhythm
           • If Narrow-Complex Tachycardia (likely SVT):
                  • Consider Vagal Maneuvers
                           • If clinical condition is sufficiently stable.
                           • If vagal maneuvers are ineffective or the patient too unstable for vagal maneuvers:
                  • Consider Adenosine
                                   • 1st dose: 0.1 mg/kg IV/IO rapid IV push, max 6 mg.
                                   • 2nd dose: 0.2 mg/kg IV/IO rapid IV push, max 12 mg.
                                   • If adenosine fails or the child deteriorates:
                  • Synchronized Cardioversion
                         • 1st Dose: 0.5–1 J/kg. If ineffective:
                         • 2nd Dose: 2 J/kg.
           • If Wide-Complex Tachycardia (likely VT):
                  • Proceed directly to synchronized cardioversion
                         • 1st Dose: 0.5–1 J/kg, If ineffective:
                         • 2nd Dose: 2 J/kg.
                  • If rhythm is regular and monomorphic and child remains stable enough, consider adenosine.
                  • Administer sedation if time and patient stability permits, but do not delay cardioversion.
   9. Patient Reassessment
       • Frequently Reassess the patient as per Universal Care CPG.
       • Specific Patient Reassessment Points:
              • Continuous ECG monitoring is required.
              • Assess for:
                    • Arrhythmias
                    • The response to any interventions (e.g., vagal maneuvers, adenosine, cardioversion), noting
                       changes in rhythm and perfusion.

Patient Safety Considerations
   • Avoid delays in cardioversion if the patient is unstable.
   • Ensure correct dosing of adenosine and monitor for transient asystole.
   • Be prepared to escalate to CPR if interventions fail.
   • Minimize patient agitation to reduce cardiac demand.

Documentation
   • Complete a comprehensive electronic Patient Care Record (ePCR), capturing the following elements to support
     continuity of care. Including:
       • Identification: Patient’s name, age, sex, weight.
       • Medical complaint: Pediatric tachycardia — specify if stable or unstable, suspected type if known (e.g., SVT, VT).
       • Information/Injuries: Relevant history: congenital heart disease, recent infection, medications, previous
         episodes, any injuries.
       • Signs:
           • Heart rate and rhythm (trends and response to treatment)
           • Blood pressure, SpO₂, mental status, skin perfusion.
       • Treatment:
           • Oxygen and airway support
           • Vagal maneuvers performed
           • Medications (e.g., adenosine — dose & response)
           • Cardioversion (energy level & response)
       • Allergies: Any known allergies
       • Medications: Medications given at scene and any relevant home meds.
       • Background: Medical history, family history if relevant.
       • Time of onset, changes during care, and special considerations (e.g., ALS back-up requested, equipment used).
       • Other: Any other relevant information.
