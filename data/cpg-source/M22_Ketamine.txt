M22                  Ketamine

 Scope of Practice         EP

 Classification          • Dissociative Anesthetic
 Usual
                         • 500mg/10ml ampule
 Presentation
 Mechanism of            • Ketamine predominantly blocks the action of glutamate on NMDA receptors,
 Action                    resulting in dose dependent analgesia, amnesia, disassociation and anesthesia.

 Indications             • Sedation of agitated patients.


                         • Known allergy
 Contraindications
                         • Significant hypertension.

                         • Active psychosis – ketamine may make this worse
 Precautions
                         • Coadministration with haloperidol or benzodiazepines (enhanced sedative effect)
                         • Laryngospasm
                         • Respiratory Depression (usually associated with large or rapid IV doses)
                         • Emergence Phenomena
 Side Effects
                         • Increase airway secretions
                         • Nystagmus
                         • Transient hypertension
                         Sedation for Abnormal Behavior
                         • IM: 1-4mg/kg (repeat after 10 minutes, once only)
                         • IV/IO: 1mg/kg bolus over 1-2minutes. Maximum dose 100mg.
                         Sedation for Agitation (e.g. ROSC/TBI/Cardioversion/Pacing)
 Adult Dose
                         • 0.25-1mg/kg IV/IO
 & Administration
                         Dilution for IV Administration
                         • In a 10ml Syringe, draw up 8mls of normal saline.
                         • Add 2ml (100mg) of Ketamine.
                         • Final concentration of the solution is 10mg/1ml
 Pediatric Dose
                         • Not for pediatric administration
 & Administration
                         • Pregnancy: Safety has not been demonstrated. Administration in pregnancy is not
 Use in Pregnancy          recommended.
 and Lactation           • Lactation: Safety has not been demonstrated. Administration in lactation is not
                           recommended.
                         • Ketamine must not be administered to facilitate intubation.
                         • Many of the adverse effects of ketamine are related to high doses or rapid IV
                           administration.
                               • Where possible slowly administer IV doses slowly.
 Additional
                         • Emergence Phenomena only requires management if it is impeding patient care.
 Information
                               • Low dose benzodiazepines may be administered if required however this
                                 may cause significant lowering of consciousness.
                               • Benzodiazepines should be administered IV to enable lower and more
                                 accurate dosages.
