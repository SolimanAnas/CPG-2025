CPG 4.2           Pediatric Basic Life Support

Patient Care Goals
   1. Recognize cardiac arrest promptly in infants and children: Quickly identify unresponsiveness, abnormal or absent
      breathing, and absence of a pulse or inadequate perfusion to initiate BLS without delay.
   2. Provide immediate, high-quality chest compressions and effective ventilations: Deliver compressions at the
      correct depth and rate, with full chest recoil, and minimize interruptions; combine with effective ventilations using
      age-appropriate techniques.
   3. Minimize interruptions in compressions and optimize oxygen delivery: Keep interruptions in compressions under
      10 seconds, prioritize maintaining adequate oxygenation through timely ventilations, and prevent hypoxia.
   4. Use defibrillation promptly if indicated: Recognize shockable rhythms early, attach an AED as soon as available,
      and deliver a shock without delay when indicated.
   5. Maintain normoxia and avoid hyperventilation: Provide just enough oxygen to achieve an SpO₂ ≥94%, avoiding
      unnecessary hyperoxia; ventilate at an appropriate rate and volume to prevent hyperventilation and maintain
      EtCO₂ in the normal range (35–45 mmHg).

Patient Inclusion & Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion Criteria
         • Infants and children ≤13 years found unresponsive, not breathing normally, and/or pulseless.
         • Infants and children with inadequate circulation require BLS interventions.
Exclusion Criteria
         • Obvious signs of irreversible death (e.g., rigor mortis, dependent lividity, decapitation).


Key Patient Assessment Points
   1. Scene Safety & Initial Impression
        • Confirm scene is safe. Assess for responsiveness using tactile and verbal stimuli.
   2. Breathing Assessment
        • Check for normal breathing (no more than 10 seconds). Gasping is not normal breathing.
   3. Circulation
        • Check central pulse (carotid in children >1 year, brachial in infants <1 year) within 10 seconds.
        • If no pulse or HR <60 bpm with poor perfusion despite oxygenation and ventilation, begin chest compressions.
   2. Determine Triage Category
          • Determine Triage Category as per Patient Triage Categories CPG.
               • All unconscious patients and patients in cardiac arrest are Red Priority.


Patient Management & Interventions
   1. Check for Responsiveness
        • Verify unresponsiveness by assessing for no response to verbal or painful stimuli (AVPU).
   2. Request ALS back-up
        • Request ALS support within 5 minutes of arrival if not already activated.
        • Specify “pediatric cardiac arrest” and confirm ETA of ALS unit.
   3. Retrieve Emergency Equipment
        • First rescuer remains with the patient.
        • Second rescuer retrieves the AED and other emergency equipment.
   3. Primary Survey
        • Open airway using head-tilt-chin-lift (if no trauma) or jaw-thrust (if trauma suspected).
        • Look for no breathing or only gasping and check pulse (simultaneously).

Patient Safety Considerations
   • Avoid excessive ventilation — target normocapnia.
   • Ensure correct compression depth & rate.
   • Minimize interruptions in CPR.
   • Use appropriate-sized equipment for infants/children.
   • Defibrillate as soon as possible if shockable rhythm detected.
   • Protect airway and cervical spine if trauma suspected.


Documentation
   • Identification: Patient’s name (if known), age, sex, estimated weight.
   • Medical complaint: Cardiac arrest — specify if witnessed, unwitnessed, or found unresponsive.
   • Information/Injuries: Relevant history (e.g., trauma, drowning, pre-existing conditions, suspected cause).
   • Signs: Initial and ongoing HR, rhythm, SpO₂, EtCO₂, skin color, responsiveness.
   • Treatment: CPR (start time, quality, depth, interruptions), defibrillation (number, energy), airway interventions,
     oxygen, glucose, medications.
   • Allergies: Any known allergies if identified.
   • Medications: Any medications administered during resuscitation.
   • Background & Other: Medical history if available, time of collapse, bystander CPR, name & designation of receiving
     hospital staff.

Key Performance Indicators
   1. Initiate CPR within 1 minute of recognition of cardiac arrest.

Clinical Insights & Additional Guidance
   • Pediatric cardiac arrest is often secondary to respiratory failure or shock; prioritize oxygenation & ventilation.
   • Use appropriate compression depth & avoid leaning on chest between compressions.
   • Ensure clear communication and delegation of tasks during resuscitation.
   • If ALS back-up is delayed and hospital is nearby, consider transport while maintaining effective CPR.
   • Consider reversible causes (4 Hs & 4 Ts) — hypoxia, hypovolemia, hypothermia, hypo/hyperkalemia, toxins,
     tamponade, tension pneumothorax, thrombosis.
