M9                   Diazepam

 Scope of Practice        EP

 Classification          • Benzodiazepine
 Usual                   • 10mg/2ml ampule
 Presentation            • 5mg/2.5ml rectal tube
 Mechanism of            • Potentiates the action of the GABA neurotransmitter producing sedation and
 Action                    seizure inhibition
                         • Seizures
 Indications
                         • Sedation of agitated or combative patients



 Contraindications       • Known allergy


                         • Respiratory distress
 Precautions
                         • Shock and/or hypotension
                         • Reduced level of consciousness
 Side Effects            • Respiratory Depression
                         • Hypotension
                         Seizures
                         • 5mg IV
                         Sedation
                         • 5mg IV
 Adult Dose
                         • Doses should be reduced if agitation is secondary to traumatic brain injury.
 & Administration
                         Dilution for IV/IO Administration
                         • In a 10ml syringe, draw up 8ml normal saline.
                         • Add 2ml (10mg) of Diazepam.
                         • Solution concentration is then 1mg/1ml.
                         Seizures
                         • 0.1mg/kg IV/IO (max 5mg)
                         • 0.5mg/kg PR (maximum 20mg)
 Pediatric Dose
                         Dilution for IV Administration
 & Administration
                         • In a 10ml syringe, draw up 8ml normal saline.
                         • Add 2ml (10mg) of Diazepam.
                         • Solution concentration is then 1mg/1ml.
                         • Pregnancy: Fetal harm has been demonstrated however, diazepam should be
 Use in Pregnancy          administered if clinically indicated.
 and Lactation           • Lactation: Safety has not been established however, diazepam should be
                           administered if clinically indicated.
                         • Diazepam is not recommended to be given IM due to its slow onset of action. Use
                           IM midazolam.
 Additional              • Benzodiazepines may cause airway, breathing and circulation compromise.
 Information             • If administered, all possible monitoring should be in place.
                         • An IV (if not available in place) should be established as soon as possible after
                           administration.
