M16                  Glucagon

 Scope of Practice       EMT

 Classification          • Hormone
 Usual
                         • 1mg/1ml vial
 Presentation
 Mechanism of            • Glucagon stimulates glycogenolysis (the breakdown of glycogen into glucose)
 Action                    predominantly in the liver, increasing blood glucose levels.
                         • Hypoglycemia
 Indications             • Beta Blocker overdose
                         • Calcium Channel Blocker Overdose

 Contraindications       • Known allergy


 Precautions             • Patients where glycogen stores may be depleted (see additional information)

                         • Reported difficulty maintaining stable glucose levels for up to two weeks following
 Side Effects
                           administration

                         Hypoglycemia
                         • 1mg IM/SC
                         Betablocker & Calcium Chanel Blocker Overdose
 Adult Dose
                         • 1mg IM.
 & Administration
                            • Administer up to 5mg IM (at different injection sites) if additional IM glucagon is
                              available.
                            • Repeat every 10-15 minutes if required.
                         Hypoglycemia
                         • >6 years (or >25kg):1mg IM/SC
                         • <6 years (or <25kg): 0.5mg IM/SC
 Pediatric Dose
                         Betablocker & Calcium Chanel Blocker Overdose
 & Administration
                         • >6 years (or >25kg): 1mg IM.
                         • <6years (or <25kg): 0.5mg IM.
                            • Repeat every 10-15 minutes if required.
                         • Pregnancy: Safety has not been established. However, glucagon should be
 Use in Pregnancy          administered if clinically indicated.
 and Lactation           • Lactation: Safety has not been established. If essential to be administered, the
                           mother should be advised to stop breast feeding until advised by their physician.
                         • Patient whose glycogen stores may already be depleted:
                                • Non-diabetics
                                • Septic patients
                                • Young children
                                • Following extreme exercise
                                • Has not eaten in more than 12 hours
 Additional
                                • Adrenal insufficiency
 Information
                                • Chronic hypoglycemia
                                • Alcohol induced hypoglycemia
                         • Glucagon is unlikely to be effective in these patients, however glucagon may be
                           considered if no IV access can be obtained, the patient remains significantly
                           hypoglycemic, and/or a reduced level of consciousness is causing airway,
                           breathing or circulatory compromise.
