CPG 6.8           Hypoglycemia

Patient Care Goals
   1. Rapidly identify and manage hypoglycemia to prevent neurological injury.
   2. Restore blood glucose levels to a safe range.
   3. Prevent hypoglycemia recurrence and ensure appropriate disposition.

Patient Inclusion & Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion Criteria
   • Patients presenting with symptoms suggestive of hypoglycemia, including:
         • Confusion, irritability, sweating, shakiness, seizures, or unresponsiveness; random blood sugar (RBS) <70
           mg/dL.
Exclusion Criteria
   • Hyperglycemia without hypoglycemic symptoms.
   • Altered mental status or symptoms clearly attributable to another cause (e.g., stroke, trauma, or drug overdose
     without glucose abnormality).
   • Patients with normal blood glucose levels and no clinical suspicion of glycemic disturbance.


Key Patient Assessment Points
   1. Glucose Measurement
        • Check RBS to confirm hypoglycemia (<70 mg/dL) .
        • Repeat RBS after interventions to monitor response.
   2. History Specific to Glycemic Disorders
        • Confirm history of diabetes (type 1 or type 2), insulin use, or oral hypoglycemic agents.
        • Inquire about recent medication compliance (e.g., excess hypoglycemic agents).
        • Identify precipitating factors (e.g., infection, stress, dietary changes).
   3. Symptoms of Hypoglycemia
        • Assess for autonomic symptoms (e.g., sweating, tremors, anxiety, palpitations).
        • Evaluate neurological symptoms (e.g., confusion, irritability, seizures, or coma).
   4. Determine Triage Category
        • Determine Triage Category as per Patient Triage Categories CPG.


Patient Management & Interventions
   1. Primary and Secondary Survey
        • Perform primary and secondary survey as per Universal Care CPG.
        • Patients who are unresponsive appropriate airway assessment and appropriate management.
        • Unconscious patients should be placed in the recovery position as a minimum while treatment is being
          administered.
        • Consider Oxygen Administration:
            • Consider oxygen if symptoms of hypoxia or respiratory distress are present.
            • Titrate to maintain SpO2 ≥94%.
   2. Consider Red Flags
        • Severe hypoglycemia (RBS <70 mg/dL with seizures, unresponsiveness, or coma).
        • Hypotension (SBP <90 mmHg in adults, or age-specific for pediatrics) or signs of shock.
        • Persistent symptoms despite initial interventions.
        • Persistent altered mental status after treatment.
        • Bradycardia, or shock.
   3. Consider ALS back-up
        • Consider the requirement for ALS back-up as per Universal Care CPG.
   4. Establish IV Access
        • For administration of IV dextrose or fluids.
        • Consider IO access if IV unsuccessful and patient is critical.
        • Avoid fluids in uncomplicated hypoglycemia unless hypotension is present.

        • Monitor for fluid overload, especially in patients with cardiac or renal comorbidities.
   5. Fluid Therapy
        • Avoid fluids in uncomplicated hypoglycemia unless hypotension is present.
        • Monitor for fluid overload, especially in patients with cardiac or renal comorbidities.
   6. Dextrose 10%
        • Adults: 100-200 mL IV (10-20 g), repeat as needed if RBS remains <70 mg/dL.
        • Pediatrics: 2-4 mL/kg IV (0.2-0.4 g/kg, max 25 g).
   7. Glucose 40% Oral Paste
        • Adults: 15-20 g orally if conscious and able to swallow.
        • Pediatrics: 0.5-1 g/kg orally (max 15 g).
   8. Glucagon
        • Indications:
              • If IV access is unavailable and patient is unresponsive
        • Adults: 1 mg IM. Once only.
        • Pediatrics: 0.5 mg IM (for <20 kg) or 1 mg (for ≥20 kg). Once only.
   9. Patient Reassessment
        • Frequently Reassess the patient as per Universal Care CPG.
        • Specific Patient Reassessment Points:
            • Glucose Reassessment
                   • Recheck RBS after each intervention.


Patient Safety Considerations
   • Ensure airway is secure before administering oral glucose.
   • Monitor closely for seizures or vomiting.
   • Reassess blood glucose after treatment to ensure response.
   • Document all treatment and timing of reassessment.


Clinical Insights & Guidance
   • Rule out alternative causes of altered mental status such as stroke, head trauma, or overdose.
   • Consider hypoglycemia as a secondary complication in seizure patients.
   • If family or caregivers administered treatment prior to EMS arrival, confirm substance and dose.
   • Patients with repeated hypoglycemia should be advised to follow up with their diabetes care provider.


Documentation
   • Identification: Full patient name, age, and gender.
   • Mechanism / Medical Complaint: Suspected or known hypoglycemia; symptoms such as confusion, tremor,
     seizure.
   • Injuries / Information: Diabetes history, insulin or oral agents, missed meals, recent illness.
   • Signs: Pre- and post-treatment RBS, AVPU or GCS, vitals, seizure activity.
   • Treatment:
         • Oral glucose, IV dextrose, or IM glucagon (dose, route, response).
         • Reassessment after treatment.
   • Allergies: Known drug allergies relevant to treatment.
   • Medications: Diabetic medications taken or missed.
   • Background: Diabetes, renal disease, past hypoglycemia episodes.
   • Other: Scene details, bystander interventions, hospital notification.


Key Performance Indicators
   1. Perform and document RBS before and after treatment.
   2. Administer glucose (as appropriate) for all patients with an RBS<70mg/dL.
