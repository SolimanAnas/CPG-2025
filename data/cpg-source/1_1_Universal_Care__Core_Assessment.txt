CPG 1.1           Universal Care

Patient Care Goals
   1. To ensure safety and stabilize the patient’s airway, breathing, and circulation.
   2. Provide a standardized, systematic approach to assess and manage all patients in the prehospital setting to
      ensure timely identification of life-threatening conditions and optimize patient outcomes.
   3. Deliver appropriate initial interventions to stabilize patients while facilitating linkage to specific clinical guidelines
      based on assessment findings.
   4. Ensure safe and efficient patient care delivery, prioritizing safety, patient comfort, and effective communication
      during transfer to definitive care with accurate documentation.

Patient Inclusion & Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion Criteria
    • All patients requiring emergency medical services, who require medical assessment and/or treatment, regardless of
      complaint or condition.
    • Patients with medical, traumatic, or undifferentiated presentations necessitating universal care principles.
Exclusion Criteria
    • None noted – Universal care guidelines apply to all patients.


Key Patient Assessment Points
   1. Scene Safety Evaluation and Initial Evaluation
          • Safety: Assess hazards (environmental, violence, chemical exposures) to ensure safety for EMS personnel,
            patients, and bystanders. Use appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE)
          • Resource Assessment: Determine the number of patients and request additional resources if needed.
            (e.g., ALS backup, mass casualty declaration).
   2. Primary Survey
          • General impression:
                 • The general impression is formed from observations on arrival to the patient.
                        • Evaluate appearance, breathing, and circulation to form a general impression.
                 • In pediatric patients, this is known as the Pediatric Assessment Triangle.
          • ABC Approach: Assess Airway, Breathing, and Circulation (ABC) to identify life threatening conditions:
                 • Ensure airway patency. (e.g., suction, airway adjuncts).
                 • Assess breathing (rate, effort, Sp02)
                 • Check pulse, control bleeding, and evaluate consciousness (AVPU or GCS 3-15)
               Adapt ABC Approach as Needed:
                 • Trauma: Use C-ABC (Catastrophic Hemorrhage/C-Spine Control, Airway, Breathing, Circulation)
                 • Cardiac Arrest: Follow CAB: Compressions, Airway, Breathing.
                 • Baseline Vitals: Record initial vital signs (pulse, BP, respiratory rate, Sp02, temperature, RBS if
                    indicated).
   3. Determine Patient Triage Category
          • Based upon findings in the primary survey, patient triage may be able to be determined. (e.g. bradycardia, or
            no breathing) as per Patient Triage Categories CPG.
          • If the triage status is unable to be determined in the primary survey, conduct a secondary survey.
   4. Secondary Survey
          • Perform a systematic head-to-toe exam to identify additional injuries or conditions.
               • For Trauma: conduct a Rapid Trauma Survey to identify hidden injuries (DCAP BLS TIC – Deformities,
                 Contusions, Abrasions, Punctures/Penetrations, Burns, Lacerations, Swelling, Tenderness, Instability,
                 Crepitus)
               • For Medical Complaints: focus on relevant systems (e.g., cardiovascular, neurological)
          • The following aspects should be examined (as appropriate to the medical condition or traumatic injury) in
            the secondary survey assessment:
               • Head
                    • Pupils

Patient Management & Interventions
   1. Primary Survey
          • Conduct a rapid ABCDE assessment (Airway, Breathing, Circulation, Disability, Exposure) to identify and
            address immediate life threats (e.g., airway obstruction, severe hemorrhage)
                • Adapt ABC Approach:
                  • Trauma: C-ABC (Catastrophic Hemorrhage/C-Spine, Airway, Breathing, Circulation)
                  • Cardiac Arrest: CAB (compressions, airway, breathing).
                • Airway: Open airway (head tilt/chin lift, jaw thrust), suction, and/or insert airway adjuncts (OPA/NPA,
                  supraglottic, ETT)
                    • Refer to Airway Management CPG for advanced airways.
                    • For patients with laryngectomies or tracheostomies, remove all objects or clothing which may
                      obstruct the opening of these devices. Maintain the flow of prescribed oxygen and reposition the
                      head and/or neck.
                • Breathing:
                    • Evaluate rate, breath sounds, accessory muscle use, retractions, patient positioning, oxygen
                      saturations.
                    • Provide supplemental oxygen as appropriate to achieve a Sp02 of 94-98% based on clinical
                      presentation and assessment of ventilation, using appropriate delivery method:
                         • Nasal cannula (2-6L/min) for mild hypoxia
                         • Non-rebreather mask (10-15L/min) for moderate to severe hypoxia.
                         • BVM for inadequate ventilation.
                         • Titrate oxygen to avoid hyperoxia, especially in COPD or suspected stroke patients.
                    • If the patient is apneic (not breathing) Go to Airway Management CPG
                • Circulation:
                    • Control any major external bleeding (direct pressure, tourniquets, hemostatic agents)
                    • Assess pulse:
                           • If no pulse, go to the appropriate Resuscitation CPG.
                           • Assess rate and quality of carotid and radial pulses
                    • Evaluate perfusion by assessing skin color and temperature
                    • Evaluate capillary refill.
                • Disability:
                    • Initiate manual cervical spine restriction if trauma is suspected, maintaining neutral alignment.
                    • Evaluate patient’s initial responsiveness using: AVPU
                    • Evaluate gross motor and sensory function in all extremities
                    • Check blood glucose in patients with altered mental status or suspected stroke. If blood glucose
                      is less than 70mg/dl - go to Hypoglycemia CPG.
                    • If acute stroke suspected: Complete BEFAST assessment and go to Stroke CPG
                • Expose patient for examination as appropriate to complaint:
                    • Be considerate of patient modesty
                    • Keep patient warm/cool as required.
   2. Consider Advanced Life Support (ALS) Back-UpA
          • Evaluate the need for ALS back-up:
              • ALS back-up should be considered when advanced skills or interventions are required to treat the
                patient.
              • ALS back-up should be requested within 5 minutes of arrival at the patient.

CPG 1.1          Universal Care

              • It is appropriate to request for ALS back-up, and later cancel the request if it is not required (e.g. the
                patient improves, or it is determined that the ambulance will reach hospital significantly faster than the
                back-up will arrive at the patient).
              • When indicated, the request for ALS back-up should be made. Do not assume that no ALS unit is
                available.
              • It is appropriate to request for ALS back-up and rendezvous with the ALS unit en route to hospital.
              • Time to Hospital:
                      • Under normal circumstances, ALS back-up should only be requested if the time for the ALS unit to
                        reach the patient is significantly faster than the time to reach hospital.
                      • As a general guideline for most ambulances, where there is a transfer time of less than 10
                        minutes, and ALS back-up has not already been dispatched: requesting for ALS back-up is usually
                        not appropriate. The patient should be transferred directly to hospital.
                           • Extrication time, the ability to adequately perform clinical skills and the potential for the
                              patient to deteriorate should also be factored into time-to-hospital decision making.
              • Unstable Red-Priority Patients:
                      • Generally, all patients categorized as critical or ‘red priority’ are unstable as they have a high
                        potential to deteriorate and should have ALS back-up requested.
              • Stable Red-Priority Patients:
                      • Some Red Priority patients, while critical, may be categorized as ‘stable-red priority’ if they:
                              • Are being adequately managed with BLS procedures, and/
                              • Have acceptable vital signs, and/
                              • Are not expected to deteriorate during transfer, and/
                              • Do not require any urgent advanced life support interventions.
                      • Examples of these cases could be controlled arterial limb bleeding, or an uncomplicated CVA.
                      • All other red priority patients should be categorized as ‘unstable’. Clinical staff must use sound
                        clinical judgement when determining if a patient may be categorized as stable.
                      • It is appropriate to not request for ALS back-up for Stable Red Priority patients.
   3. Obtain a full set of baseline vital signs
         • This includes: pulse, blood pressure, respiratory rate, temperature, Sp02, Blood glucose measurement (if
           indicated), EtC02 (if indicated), and neurologic status assessment.
         • Neurologic status assessment: establish a baseline and reassess for any change in patient neurologic
           status
                • Assess AVPU (Alert, responds to Voice, responds only to Pain, Unresponsive)
                • Assess Glasgow Coma Score (GCS).
         • Patients with cardiac or respiratory compromise:
                • Pulse oximetry
                • 12-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) should be obtained promptly in patients with cardiac or suspected
                   cardiac complaints.
                • Continuous cardiac monitoring
                • Consider waveform capnography for patients with respiratory complaints (essential for critical
                   patients and those patients who require invasive airway management)
         • Patients with altered mental status:
                • Check blood glucose. If <70mg/dl go to Hypoglycemia CPG.
   4. Secondary Survey
         • If interventions are required in the primary survey, it may not be possible to complete the secondary survey.
         • Neurologic status assessment:
                • Establish a baseline and reassess for any change in patient neurologic status.
                • Assess Glasgow Coma Score (GCS).
         • Physical Examination
                • Perform a physical examination tailored to the patient’s chief complaint or suspected condition,
                   avoiding delays in critical patients.

   5. Consider Red Flags
          • Consider findings which may indicate a threat to life or require immediate intervention, such as:

                 • Altered mental status (AVPU: Voice, Pain, Unresponsive) or neurological deficits (e.g., stroke

                 • Hypoxia (SpO2 <94%), hypotension (SBP <90 mmHg), or abnormal vital signs indicating instability.
                 • High-risk mechanisms (e.g., fall >10 feet, high-speed crash, electrocution) suggestive of severe
                   injuries.
   6. Vascular Access
          • Establish IV access if indicated or in patients who:
              • Are at risk for clinical deterioration, or
              • Require IV fluid or medication administration.
          • Intraosseous (IO) access is indicated if IV access fails or is impractical.
          • Monitor for complications (e.g., infiltration, dislodgement) and secure access properly.
   7. Temperature Management
          • Monitor for hypo/hyperthermia, especially in environmental emergencies, severe trauma and sepsis.
          • Use passive warming or cooling measures as needed.
   8. Pain Management
          • Administer appropriate analgesia per Pain Management CPG.
   9. Patient ReassessmentB
          • Patient Reassessment is required every:
                 • Every 5 minutes for critical (red-priority) patients
                 • Every 10 minutes for serious (yellow-priority) patients
                 • Every 15 minutes for non-critical (green-priority) patients.
          • Reassessment should include:
                 • Level of consciousness, airway, breathing, and circulation.
                 • Vital signs
                       • Routine Blood Glucose reassessment is not required for patients with:
                            • Acceptable blood glucose levels, and/
                            • No clinical suspicion that hypo or hyperglycemia requires reassessment.
                 • Condition specific signs and symptoms
                 • Any interventions (e.g. IV line patency)
                 • Response to interventions. (e.g. pain level)
                 • Monitoring of trends to detect changes in patient condition.
   10. Consider Tele-EMS Consultation
          • Tele-EMS should be consulted for complex or deteriorating conditions.
   11. Determine Receiving Facility
          • Consider the appropriate hospital based on the patient condition (e.g., trauma center for severe injuries,
            stroke center for suspected stroke).
                 • Confirm receiving facility with Dispatch 2 prior to departing.

Patient Safety Considerations
   • Respect the patients' rights:
          • All patients above the age of 18 years old have the right to refuse treatment and transfer to hospital provided
             they have full mental capacity.
           • Refer to Refusal of Transfer CPG.
   • For a patient to have full mental capacity, they must be able to understand, retain and repeat information told to
     them by you.
   • Advanced Care Directives/ Living Will’s are not recognized in the prehospital environment in Dubai at the time of
     publication of this CPG version.
   • Evaluate the need for ALS backup or Tele EMS consultation for complex or deteriorating conditions.
   • Consider air medical transport, if available, for patients with time-critical conditions where ground transport
     exceeds 30 minutes.

Documentation
   • Complete a comprehensive electronic Patient Care Record (ePCR), capturing the following elements to support
     continuity of care. Including:
           • Identification: Patient’s full name, age, and gender.
           • Mechanism / Medical Complaint: Primary complaint or mechanism of injury/illness prompting EMS
             activation.
           • Injuries / Information: Relevant assessment findings, red flags, risk factors, vital signs, and initial survey
             details.
           • Signs: Vital signs, AVPU/GCS, pain score (if relevant), and changes during reassessment.
           • Treatment: All interventions performed, including oxygen therapy, IV/IO access, medications, repositioning,
             and reassessments.
           • Allergies: Any known allergies identified or confirmed during patient history.
           • Medications: Current prescribed or over-the-counter medications, especially those affecting current
             condition.
           • Background: Past medical history, recent health events, or factors influencing presentation or care
             decisions.
           • Other: Scene observations and communication with receiving facility.
   • Ensure timestamps for all critical events, assessments, interventions, and reassessments to establish a clear
     timeline for care.

Clinical Insights & Additional Guidance
   • Pediatrics
        • Pediatrics in Dubai are considered less than or equal to 13 years of age.
        • Use length-based tape (Broselow Tape) to estimate patient weight and guide medication therapy and adjunct
           choice.
   • Geriatrics
        • Geriatrics in Dubai are considered greater than or equal to 65 years of age.
        • In geriatric patients, as well as all adult patients, reduced medication dosages may apply to patients with renal
           disease (i.e., on dialysis or a diagnosis of chronic renal insufficiency) or hepatic disease (i.e., severe cirrhosis
           or end-stage liver disease)
   • Comorbidities
         • Reduced medication dosages may apply to patients with renal disease (i.e., on dialysis or a diagnosis of
           chronic renal insufficiency) or hepatic disease (i.e., severe cirrhosis or end-stage liver disease)
   • Cultural and Psychological Sensitivity
         • Communicate respectfully, considering language barriers and cultural barriers.
         • Provide reassurance and address patient/family anxiety to improve compliance and trust.
   • Infection Control
         • Adhere to standard precautions (e.g., PPE, hand hygiene) to prevent cross-contamination.
         • Consider enhanced precautions for suspected infectious diseases (e.g., TB, MRSA)
   • Scene Management
         • Co-ordinate with other emergency services (e.g., civil defense, police) to ensure efficient patient care and
           scene safety.
         • Consider extrication needs for trauma patients trapped in vehicles or confined spaces.
   • Critical Patients
         • Ideally, one to two clinicians should be assigned to exclusively monitor and facilitate patient-focused care
           where possible.
         • Apart from lifesaving interventions needed to prevent deterioration, treatment should begin as soon as
           possible but must not slow down extrication or delay transport to definitive care.
