CPG 2.2            Foreign Body Airway Obstruction

Patient Care Goals
    1. Removal of foreign body through the most appropriate means.
    2. Early activation of ALS back-up (if indicated)

Patient Inclusion & Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion Criteria
    • Mild choking (partial obstruction) where patient can breathe, cough, or speak effectively, with mild respiratory
      distress (e.g., coughing).
    • Signs of severe airway obstruction (stridor, ineffective cough, severe respiratory distress).
    • Unresponsive patient with suspected foreign body obstruction.
Exclusion Criteria
    • Patients without a history of foreign body ingestion.


Key Patient Assessment Points
    1. Primary Survey
           • Conduct a primary assessment as to Universal Care CPG to evaluate airway, breathing, circulation.
    2. Identify Airway Obstruction
           • Look for signs of foreign body airway obstruction, including; choking, noisy breathing, stridor, inability to
             speak, or ineffective ventilation.
           • Assess respiratory status: Determine if the patient can breathe, cough, or speak effectively.
    3. Determine Triage Category
           • Determine Triage Category as per Patient Triage Categories CPG.
           • All Moderate and Severe Foreign Body Airway Obstructions should be considered Red Priority.


Treatment and Interventions
    1. Consider ALS back-up
        • Consider the requirement for ALS back-up as per Universal Care CPG.
           • Severe respiratory distress or impending respiratory failure.
           • A foreign body is observed in the airway and cannot be removed.
    2. Mild Choking (Partial Obstruction):
           • Criteria:
                 • Patient can breathe, cough, or speak effectively, with mild respiratory distress (e.g., coughing).
           • Management:
                 • Encourage coughing if the patient can cough effectively.
                 • Observe closely for progression to severe obstruction, using attached monitors (SpO2, ECG) to assess
                   respiratory status and oxygenation.
                 • Avoid interventions unless symptoms worsen.
                 • Request ALS back-up for patients with worsening symptoms.
                 • Monitor continuously: Monitor SpO2 and ECG for signs of deterioration (e.g., SpO2 <92%, increased
                   respiratory effort).
                 • Reassess frequently.
    3. Severe Choking (Complete or Near-Complete Obstruction):
           • Criteria: Inability to speak, breathe, or cough effectively; severe respiratory distress (e.g., silent chest,
             cyanosis, unconsciousness).
           • Management:
                 • Request ALS back-up (back-up can be cancelled if not required).
                 • Conscious Patient:
                         • Position the patient bent-over with head lower than shoulders (if safe and feasible).
                         • Deliver up to 5 back blows between the shoulder blades, then up to 5 chest thrusts (avoid
                           abdominal thrusts to prevent abdominal injury; the Heimlich maneuver is no longer
                           recommended).

   4. Patient Monitoring & Reassessment
       • Frequently Reassess the patient as per Universal Care CPG.
           • Specific Patient Reassessment Points:
               • Reassess after each intervention for airway patency or worsening symptoms.
               • Continuously reassess the patient’s airway status, breathing, and response to treatment using
                 attached monitors (SpO2, EtCO2, ECG).

Patient Safety Considerations
   • Patient populations at risk
       • Young children, older adults and people with intellectual disabilities, as they are more prone to FBAO. Adjust
         assessment, monitoring and interventions for these groups; consider potential communication or compliance
         challenges.

Clinical Insights & Additional Information
   • Esophageal obstruction
       • If a foreign object is suspected in the esophagus (e.g., discomfort or difficulty swallowing saliva), offer small
         amounts of liquid to assist passage, but transport to the closest appropriate hospital if unsuccessful. Avoid
         airway interventions unless airway compromise is present.
   • Foreign body ingestion
       • For ingested objects in young children, assess for excessive saliva, difficulty swallowing, or abdominal pain.
         Transport urgently to the closest most appropriate surgical-capable hospital if the object is hazardous (e.g.,
         batteries, magnets, sharp items, especially button batteries due to severe injury risk).

Documentation
   • Complete a comprehensive electronic Patient Care Record (ePCR), capturing the following elements to support
     continuity of care. Including:
           • Identification: Full patient name, age, and gender.
           • Mechanism / Medical Complaint: Presenting symptoms (e.g., choking, stridor), initial airway status.
           • Injuries / Information: Relevant history (e.g., ingestion, trauma, substance use).
           • Signs:
               • Full vital signs including pulse, respiratory rate, SpO2, EtCO2.
               • Two sets of vital signs unless critical patient condition precludes.
               • Airway assessment findings (e.g., patency, obstruction).
           • Treatment:
               • Airway management steps and oxygen administration.
               • All interventions: back blows, chest thrusts, CPR, foreign body removal attempts, and response.
               • Justification if interventions were withheld.
