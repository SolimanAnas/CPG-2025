CPG 9.2           Pediatric Bradycardia

Patient Care Goals
   • Recognize and promptly treat symptomatic bradycardia to prevent progression to cardiac arrest.
   • Identify and address reversible causes.
   • Support oxygenation, ventilation, and perfusion.


Patient Inclusion & Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion Criteria
    • Age: Infants and children ≤13 years.
    • Heart rate below normal for age, with associated signs of poor perfusion:
            • Altered mental status.
            • Pallor, cyanosis, weak pulses, prolonged capillary refill.
            • Hypotension or respiratory failure.
    • Bradycardia due to reversible causes:
            • Hypoxia, vagal stimulation, hypothermia, toxins, heart block.
    • Unstable bradycardia: Not tolerating low HR, progressing to shock or cardiac arrest.
Exclusion Criteria
    • Heart rate appropriate for age without signs of hypoperfusion.
    • Known congenital heart disease with stable baseline bradycardia without deterioration.


Key Patient Assessment Points
   1. Initial impression
           • Assess responsiveness, appearance, and distress using the Pediatric Assessment Triangle (PAT): tone,
             interactiveness, breathing effort, skin color.
           • Look for signs of instability: lethargy, cyanosis, poor perfusion.
   2. Airway & Breathing Assessment
           • Ensure airway is patent and assess for respiratory distress or failure.
           • Look for hypoxia, apnea, or shallow respirations, the most common cause of bradycardia.

   3. Circulation Assessment
          • Palpate central and peripheral pulses for rate, rhythm, and quality.
          • Check capillary refill time (>3 seconds suggests poor perfusion).
          • Measure blood pressure if possible.
          • Look for skin signs: pallor, mottling, cyanosis.

   5.   ECG Monitoring
            • Apply ECG to identify rhythm (e.g., sinus bradycardia, heart block).
   6.   Assess for Reversible Causes
            • Such as; hypoxia, hypovolemia, hypothermia, toxins/drugs, increased vagal tone, or elevated intracranial
              pressure.
   7.   Patient History
            • Assess for alternative pathology or events which may guide treatment, such as; recent choking, respiratory
              infection, toxin/medication ingestion, congenital heart disease, prior episodes.
   8.   Level of consciousness
            • Assess alertness, confusion, lethargy, or unresponsiveness, indicating cerebral hypoperfusion.
   2.   Determine Triage Category
            • Determine Triage Category as per Patient Triage Categories CPG.
            • Red Priority:
                    • Bradycardia with poor perfusion, altered mental status, hypotension, or impending cardiac arrest.
                    • Requires immediate intervention.
            • Yellow Priority:
                    • Bradycardia with stable perfusion, normal mental status, and no signs of shock.
                    • Continue close monitoring and be prepared to escalate if deterioration occurs.


Patient Management & Interventions
   1. Request ALS back-up
       • Request ALS back-up as per Universal Care CPG.
       • ALS back-up should be routinely requested for pediatric patients with bradycardia.
   1. Maintain a Calm, Systematic Approach
         • Minimize stress and keep the child warm.
         • Allow caregiver to stay close if it helps calm the child.
   2. Support Oxygenation & Ventilation
         • Ensure a patient airway.
         • Administer high-flow oxygen to maintain Spo2 >94%.
         • If respiratory effort is inadequate or apnea is present, start bag-valve-mask ventilation with 100% oxygen.

           • Correct hypoxia promptly, most pediatric bradycardia is secondary to hypoxia.
   3. Start CPR if Indicated
           • If HR <60 bpm with signs of poor perfusion despite oxygenation & ventilation, begin chest compressions
             immediately.
           • Use appropriate compression depth and ratio for age (15:2 if two rescuers, 30:2 if alone).
   4. Obtain IV/IO access
           • Establish intravenous or intraosseous access as soon as possible.
   5. Administer Adrenaline (Epinephrine)
           • If bradycardia persists despite adequate oxygenation & ventilation:
                  • IV/IO: 0.01 mg/kg of 1:10,000 (0.1 mL/kg) every 3–5 minutes as needed.
                  • If no IV/IO access, via ETT: 0.1 mg/kg of 1:1,000 diluted in 3–5 mL saline.
   6. Administer Atropine
           • If vagal-mediated or suspected heart block:
                  • Dose: Atropine 0.02 mg/kg IV/IO(minimum 0.1 mg; maximum single dose 0.5 mg).
                  • May repeat once after 3–5 minutes.
                  • Maximum total dose: 1 mg in children, 3 mg in adolescents.
   7. Advanced Airway
           • Prepare for endotracheal intubation if respiratory failure persists or airway is not maintained.
   8. Consider Transcutaneous Pacing
           • If refractory to oxygenation, ventilation, chest compressions, epinephrine, and atropine, and if equipment
             and physician or advance paramedic are available, consider transcutaneous pacing.
   9. Identify & Treat Reversible Causes
           • Hypoxia → oxygen & ventilation.
           • Hypothermia → rewarming.
           • Toxins → antidotes if appropriate.
           • Vagal stimulation → remove stimulus.
   10. If Pulselessness Develops:
           • Go to Pediatric Cardiac Arrest CPG.
   11. Patient Reassessment
           • Frequently Reassess the patient as per Universal Care CPG.
           • Specific Patient Reassessment Points:
                • Continuous ECG monitoring is required:
                     • Reassess for profound bradycardia, cardiac arrest, or worsening perfusion.
                     • Be prepared to resume or escalate resuscitation efforts if condition worsens.


Patient Safety Considerations
   • Avoid excessive ventilation, maintain normocapnia.
   • Aim to maintain normocapnia, with an end-tidal CO₂ (EtCO₂) between 35–45 mmHg (4.6–6 kPa).
   • Minimize delays in initiating chest compressions when indicated.
   • Use appropriate drug dosing based on weight, the use of Braslow scale
   • Recognize bradycardia may indicate impending cardiac arrest in children.


Documentation
   • Complete a comprehensive electronic Patient Care Record (ePCR), capturing the following elements to support
    continuity of care. Including:
           • Identification: Patient’s age, sex, weight.
           • Mechanism/Medical complaint: Suspected cause of bradycardia.
           • Injuries/Information: Associated findings, vital signs.
           • Signs: Heart rate, blood pressure, SpO₂, mental status.
           • Treatment: Oxygen, medications, CPR, airway interventions.
           • Allergies: Known drug or food allergies.

Key Performance Indicators
   1. Start CPR for unconscious patients with HR <60bpm.
   2. Administer oxygen for patients with SpO₂ <94%.

Clinical Insights & Additional Guidance
   • Pediatric bradycardia is usually secondary to hypoxia, ensure oxygenation and ventilation first.
   • Bradycardia may precede cardiac arrest , act promptly.
   • Consult TeleEMS if in doubt, if the patient does not respond to initial measures, or if advanced interventions (e.g.,
     pacing) may be needed.
   • Distinguish between stable sinus bradycardia and symptomatic, unstable bradycardia requiring intervention.
