CPG 6.1           Abdominal Pain

Patient Care Goals
   1. Identify life-threatening causes of abdominal pain
   2. Improve patient comfort

Patient Inclusion & Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion Criteria
    • Abdominal pain or discomfort, related to a non-traumatic cause
Exclusion Criteria
    • Abdominal pain due to trauma
    • Abdominal pain related to pregnancy


Key Patient Assessment Points
   1. Abdominal Examination
       • Inspect for distension, bruising, or visible abnormalities.
       • Palpate for tenderness, rigidity, or masses.
       • Note rebound tenderness or guarding.
   2. Patient history
       • Use OPQRST and SAMPLE history to identity likely cause.
       • Investigate patient history to exclude possibility for pregnancy.
   3. Determine Triage Category
       • Determine Triage Category as per Patient Triage Categories CPG.


Patient Management & Interventions
   1. Primary and Secondary Survey
        • Perform primary and secondary survey as per Universal Care CPG.
   2. Consider ALS back-up
        • Consider the requirement for ALS back-up as per Universal Care CPG.
   3. Consider Red Flags
        • Severe, unrelenting pain
        • Fever (>38°C)
        • Persistent vomiting or hematemesis
        • Suspected pregnancy complications (e.g., ectopic pregnancy, miscarriage)
   4. Evaluate for Signs of Shock or Instability
        • Hypotension (SBP <90 mmHg in adults, or age-specific for pediatrics)
        • Tachycardia (HR >100 in adults, or age-specific for pediatrics)
        • Altered mental status
        • Poor perfusion (cool, clammy skin, delayed capillary refill)
   5. Assess for 12-Lead ECG Requirement
        • Perform 12-Lead ECG for patients who:
              • Are greater than 40 years of age, and/
              • Has 3 or more cardiac risk factors, including:
                      • Smoking (current or cessation within last 3 months)
                      • Obesity
                      • Diabetes
                      • Hypertension
                      • High cholesterol
                      • Cardiovascular disease
                      • Family history of cardiovascular disease
   6. Establish IV Access
          • Indicated for dehydration, hypotension, or anticipated medication administration
   7. Fluid Resuscitation

Patient Safety Considerations
   • Transport/Transfer Considerations:
         • Abdominal pain can constitute a medical emergency which is poorly detected in the ambulance setting.
         • All patients with non-specific abdominal pain or persistent vomiting should be recommended to see a doctor
              as soon as possible (if transport to hospital is refused).
            • Patients with early signs of shock, unstable vitals, or a serious cause of abdominal pain is suspected (e.g.
              appendicitis) or a serious cause cannot be ruled out should be routinely transported to hospital.

Clinical Insights & Additional Guidance
   • Common causes: gastroenteritis, appendicitis, bowel obstruction, pancreatitis, kidney stones, ectopic pregnancy,
     DKA, or mesenteric ischemia.
   • Possible Alternative causes: Myocardial infarction, Pneumonia/Pulmonary Embolus, Pregnancy (including ectopic),
     CHF, Aortic dissection, Aortic aneurysm, Peptic ulcer disease, Kidney stones, Pancreatitis /Gallbladder disease,
     Bowel obstruction, GI Bleeding, Appendicitis.

Documentation
   • Identification: Full patient name, age, and gender.
   • Mechanism / Medical Complaint: Abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting – include duration, severity, and any relieving or
        aggravating factors.
   • Injuries / Information: Relevant history – recent food intake, medications, bowel habits, associated symptoms (e.g.,
     fever, diarrhea, hematemesis).
   • Signs: Vital signs, abdominal exam findings (tenderness, guarding, rebound), mental status.
   • Treatment: Medications given (e.g., metoclopramide, paracetamol), IV fluids, pain scale before and after
     intervention.
   • Allergies: Particularly medication-related (e.g., antiemetics or analgesics).
   • Medications: Current prescribed or OTC medications.
   • Background: History of GI disease, renal issues, diabetes, prior surgeries.
   • Other: ECG findings if performed, pregnancy status if applicable, transport rationale.


Key Performance Indicators
   1. Assess and document initial and post-treatment pain score.
