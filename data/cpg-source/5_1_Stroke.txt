CPG 5.1           Stroke

Patient Care Goals
   1. Rapidly identify suspected acute stroke or transient ischemic attack (TIA) to facilitate time-sensitive transfer.
   2. Minimize secondary brain injury through supportive care.
   3. Ensure rapid transport to a stroke-capable medical facility for definitive evaluation and treatment within the
      therapeutic window.

Patient Inclusion & Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion Criteria
    • Patients presenting with signs and symptoms suggestive of acute stroke or TIA, including:
          • Sudden onset of focal neurological deficits (e.g., weakness, numbness, or paralysis of face, arm, or leg).
          • Speech difficulties (e.g., slurred speech, aphasia, or inability to speak).
          • Vision changes (e.g., sudden loss of vision, double vision).
          • Dizziness, loss of balance, or coordination problems.
          • Severe headache with no known cause (e.g., suggestive of hemorrhagic stroke).
    • Symptoms consistent with stroke assessment tools (e.g., BEFAST).
Exclusion Criteria
    • Symptoms clearly attributable to another cause (e.g., hypoglycemia, seizure, or trauma) without stroke-like features.
    • Patients with resolved symptoms and no ongoing neurological deficits (e.g., prior TIA with no new symptoms).

Key Patient Assessment Points
   1. Stroke Screening
        • Perform a ‘BEFAST” Stroke Assessment:
              • Balance: Sudden loss of balance
              • Eyes: Vision Loss
              • Face: Check for facial drooping or asymmetry.
              • Arms: Assess for unilateral weakness or drift.
              • Speech: Evaluate for slurred speech or inability to speak clearly.
              • Time: Determine the exact time of symptom onset or last known well.
   2. Neurological Deficit Assessment
        • Identify focal neurological signs (e.g., unilateral weakness, sensory loss, or neglect).
        • Assess for gaze deviation, pupil asymmetry, or facial paralysis.
        • Use AVPU (Alert, Verbal, Painful, Unresponsive) or Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) to quantify level of
          consciousness.
   3. Time of Onset
        • Confirm the precise time symptoms began or when the patient was last known well (critical for thrombolytic or
          endovascular therapy eligibility).
   4. Glucose Assessment
        • Check random blood sugar (RBS) to rule out hypoglycemia (<70 mg/dL) or hyperglycemia (>400 mg/dL), which
          can mimic stroke symptoms.
   5. Stroke Mimics
        • Consider Stoke Mimics:
              • Intracerebral lesions (e.g. tumor, abscess)
              • Seizures and post-ictal states (Todd's paralysis)
              • Hemiplegic migraine
              • Electrolyte abnormalities (e.g. Hyponatremia)
              • Conversion disorder (acute psycho-social stressors)
   6. Determine Triage Category
          • Determine Triage Category as per Patient Triage Categories CPG.
              • All patients with signs of acute stroke are Red Priority.

Adult Patient Management & Interventions
   1. Primary & Secondary Survey
        • Perform Primary and Secondary Surveys as per Universal Care CPG.
   2. Consider ALS back-up
        • Consider the requirement for ALS back-up as per Universal Care CPG.
   3. Patient Positioning
        • Patients should be positioned semi-recumbent or with the head elevated 15-30° if alert.
   4. Consider Oxygen
        • Administer to maintain SpO2 ≥94% if hypoxia is present.
        • Avoid routine oxygen administration in normoxic patients.
   5. Consider Red Flags
        • Onset of symptoms within 4.5 hours of arrival (thrombolytic window) or up to 24 hours (endovascular therapy
          window).
        • Rapidly deteriorating neurological status (e.g., worsening GCS, loss of consciousness).
        • Signs of increased intracranial pressure (e.g., unequal pupils, severe headache, vomiting).
        • Respiratory distress or hypoxia (SpO2 <90%).
        • Suspected hemorrhagic stroke (e.g., sudden severe headache, seizures).
   6. 12-Lead ECG
        • Perform a 12-lead ECG. However, do not delay transfer to acquire a 12-lead ECG if acute stroke is suspected,
          perform the 12-Lead en route to hospital.
   7. Establish IV Access
        • Indicated for patients requiring fluid administration or at risk of deterioration.
        • A large bore IV cannula (18g or larger) in a large peripheral vein is preferred, when possible. An18g cannula (or
          larger) is required for administration of contrast CT scans.
        • Do not delay time on scene to establish IV access.
   8. Patient Reassessment
        • Frequently Reassess the patient as per Universal Care CPG.


Patient Safety Considerations
   • Time-Sensitive Transport
        • Patients with Stroke symptoms with an onset <4.5 hours should be rapidly transported to a stroke center.
        • Clearly communicate with Dispatch 2 identifying stroke symptoms and symptom onset time.
   • Time of Onset
        • It is imperative that the last known well (LKW) time is accurately identified — the moment the patient was last
           known normal— as this dictates eligibility for interventions and will determine the correct hospital for the
           patient.

Clinical Insights & Additional Guidance
   • Blood Pressure Management
        • Blood pressure should not be lowered unless it exceeds 220/120 mmHg with signs of end organ dysfunction.
           This preserves cerebral perfusion in ischemic stroke, where autoregulation is impaired.
         • Tele-EMS should be contacted for further advice on blood pressure management of exceptionally hypertensive
           patients.

Documentation
   • Identification: Patient’s full name, age, and gender.
   • Mechanism / Medical Complaint: Sudden onset of neurological deficits, BEFAST symptoms, or witnessed stroke-like
     event.
   • Injuries / Information: Stroke mimics considered and ruled out (e.g., glucose level, seizure history), presence of risk
     factors (e.g., hypertension, diabetes).

   • Signs: Neurological exam findings (e.g., facial droop, limb weakness, slurred speech), GCS, pupil findings, vital

         • Time of symptom onset or last known well.

         • Scene findings and witnesses’ statements.
         • Accurate documentation of symptom timeline.
         • Communication with Dispatch and receiving hospital.


Key Performance Indicators
   1. Perform and document a BEFAST assessment for all suspected stroke cases.
   2. Perform an RBS for all patients with suspected stroke.
   3. Initiate transport all patients with stroke symptoms <4.5 hours from onset are transported within 15 minutes of
      EMS arrival.
