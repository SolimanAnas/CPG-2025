CPG 6.6           Epistaxis

Patient Care Goals
   1. Control acute epistaxis to prevent airway compromise or significant blood loss.

Patient Inclusion & Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion Criteria
    • Patients presenting with epistaxis (nosebleed), including:
          • Active nasal bleeding, unilateral or bilateral.
          • Symptoms ranging from mild, self-limiting bleeding to severe, persistent hemorrhage.
          • Associated symptoms such as dizziness, weakness, or pallor due to blood loss.
    • Suspected underlying causes such as hypertension, trauma, coagulopathy, or medication use (e.g.,
      anticoagulants).
Exclusion Criteria
    • Patients with no active bleeding or resolved epistaxis prior to arrival.


Key Patient Assessment Points
   1. Patient History
        • Determine onset, duration, and frequency of bleeding (e.g., first episode or recurrent).
        • Identify potential triggers (e.g., nasal irritation, trauma, recent surgery, or dry environment).
        • Inquire about medication use (e.g., anticoagulants, antiplatelets, or nasal sprays).
        • Confirm history of bleeding disorders, hypertension, or recent nasal procedures.
   2. Bleeding
        • Assess the severity of bleeding (e.g., dripping, streaming, or posterior bleeding with blood in the throat).
        • Note the site of bleeding (anterior vs. posterior, unilateral vs. bilateral).
        • Evaluate for signs of significant blood loss (e.g., pallor, tachycardia, or hypotension).
   3. Airway and Swallowing Assessment
        • Check for blood pooling in the oropharynx or difficulty swallowing, indicating posterior epistaxis.
        • Assess for airway compromise due to blood aspiration or clot obstruction.
   4. Determine Triage Category
        • Determine Triage Category as per Patient Triage Categories CPG.


Patient Management & Interventions
   1. Primary Survey
        • Perform primary survey as per Universal Care CPG.
   2. Secondary Survey
        • Conduct a focused head and neck examination to assess the nasal cavity and oropharynx.
   3. Consider Red Flags
        • Persistent or profuse bleeding unresponsive to initial interventions.
        • Signs of hypovolemic shock (e.g., SBP <90 mmHg in adults, tachycardia, pallor).
        • Airway compromise due to posterior bleeding or clot aspiration.
        • Known coagulopathy or anticoagulant use with uncontrolled bleeding.
        • Recurrent epistaxis with history of nasal surgery or malignancy.
   4. Evaluate for Signs of Shock or Instability
        • Hypotension (SBP <90 mmHg in adults, or age-specific for pediatrics), tachycardia, or poor perfusion (e.g.,
          cool, clammy skin, delayed capillary refill >2 seconds).
        • If shock is present, initiate fluid resuscitation and prioritize bleeding control.
   5. Bleeding Control Interventions
        • Positioning
               • If the cause of epistaxis is not related to trauma, position the patient upright with the head tilted forward
                 to prevent blood pooling in the oropharynx.
               • Instruct the patient to avoid swallowing blood or blowing their nose.
        • Nasal Compression

                • Apply firm, continuous pressure to the soft part of the nose (alae nasi) for 10-15 minutes.
                • Use a gauze pad or cloth to absorb blood and protect skin.
         • Nasal Packing
              • Consider nasal packing with hemostatic agents or gauze for persistent anterior bleeding.
              • Posterior packing requires hospital management; prioritize transport if suspected.
   6. Establish IV Access
        • Indicated for hypotension, significant blood loss, or anticipated fluid administration.
        • Consider intraosseous (IO) access if IV access cannot be obtained
   7. Fluid Resuscitation
          • Adults:
                • If SBP is less than 90 mmHg or HR greater than 120 BPM:
                     • Consider 500 mL bolus of Normal Saline or Lactated Ringers IV/IO.
                     • If SBP greater than 90 mmHg and heart rate less than 120 BPM, no IV fluids are required.
                     • Repeat 500mL IV fluid bolus’ as needed to maintain a SPB of 90mmHg
                     • Maximum total IV/IO fluid bolus 2000mL.
          • Pediatrics:
                • If tachycardic for age, AND signs of poor perfusion (hypotension for age, greater than 2-second
                  capillary refill, altered mental status, hypoxia, weak pulses, pallor, or mottled/cool skin):
                       • Administer 20 mL/kg IV/IO crystalloid bolus and reassess.
                       • Repeat 20 mL/kg IV/IO fluid bolus’ as needed if signs of shock remain.
                       • Maximum total IV/IO fluid bolus of 60mL/kg.
                • Fluid administration target is normal blood pressure for patient age.
   8. Consider ALS back-up
          • Consider the requirement for ALS back-up as per Universal Care CPG.
          • Transport should be prioritized for uncontrolled bleeding. Do not wait on scene for ALS, meet the ALS unit en
            route to hospital if ALS back-up is required.
   9. Patient Reassessment
        • Frequently Reassess the patient as per Universal Care CPG.
        • Specific Patient Reassessment Points:
              • Bleeding:
                  • Reassess bleeding control every 5-10 minutes during interventions.
                     • If bleeding continues maintain direct pressure.
                     • Monitor for signs of airway compromise or worsening blood loss.


Patient Safety Considerations
   • Avoid delays in transport for patients with red flags (e.g., posterior epistaxis, significant airway).


Clinical Insights & Additional Guidance
   • Epistaxis with uncontrolled bleeding may result in severe hypovolemic shock. When initial attempts to stop bleeding
     fail, prioritize rapid transfer to hospital.

Documentation
   • Identification: Full patient name, age, and gender.
   • Mechanism / Medical Complaint: Nature and onset of bleeding, associated symptoms (e.g., dizziness, pallor).
   • Injuries / Information: History of epistaxis, hypertension, trauma, anticoagulant use, or nasal surgery.
   • Signs: Vital signs, site of bleeding (anterior/posterior), evidence of airway compromise or blood in oropharynx.
   • Treatment:
         • Patient positioning and nasal compression performed.
         • Nasal packing used: type and response.
         • IV fluids given: type, volume, and reassessment findings.
         • ALS backup or hospital transport arranged.
