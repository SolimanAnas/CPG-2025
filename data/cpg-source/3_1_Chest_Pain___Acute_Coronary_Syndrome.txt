CPG 3.1          Chest Pain / Acute Coronary Syndrome

Patient Care Goals
   1. Conduct a full vital signs assessment and 12-Lead ECG to quickly identify ST-elevation myocardial infarction
      (STEMI) and/or Occlusion Myocardial Infarction (OMI).
   2. Determine the time of symptom onset.
   3. Expedite patient transport to an appropriate cardiac hospital.

Patient Inclusion & Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion Criteria
    • Patients presenting with ischemic‐type symptoms, with or without ECG changes suggestive of ischemia.
          • Ischemic-type symptoms include, but not limited to:
               • Chest pain / pressure or discomfort
               • Radiation to arms, jaw, back, shoulders
               • Dyspnea / shortness of breath
               • Diaphoresis / sweating
               • Nausea, vomiting
               • Dizziness, syncope, palpitations
    • Identified ACS on 12-lead ECG:
          • STEMI
          • Non-STEMI / ischemic ECG changes (e.g., T wave inversions, ST depressions, new LBBB)
    • Patients with non-specific chest pain or atypical presentation when cardiac origin cannot be excluded.
    • Presentations of epigastric discomfort, syncope, or palpitations when a cardiac cause is among differential,
      and no clear alternative non-cardiac cause is evident at first evaluation.
    • Patients with signs of hemodynamic compromise or arrhythmias, even if chest pain is absent, if suspicion of
      an acute cardiac etiology is high.
Exclusion Criteria
    • Patients with a clear non-cardiac cause of chest pain.


Key Patient Assessment Points
   1. Conduct a Full Vital Signs Assessment
         • Perform a 12-Lead ECG.
         • Consider right-sided and posterior ECG.
   2. Determine Time of Symptom Onset
         • Determine the time of symptom onset as this will influence patient management.
   3. Evaluate and determine pain type
         • Investigate chest pain using OPQRST assessment and associated signs and symptoms.
         • Determine between ischemic, non-ischemic and non-cardiac pain.
   4. Determine Triage Category
         • Determine Triage Category as per Patient Triage Categories CPG.
             • Red Priority:
                 • Signs of Severe Shock
                 • ST elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI)
                 • Chest Pain or Dysrhythmias or other ECG changes WITH significant cardiovascular compromise.

Patient Management and Interventions
   1. Consider Oxygen Therapy
          • Target SpO2 of 90%.
          • Administer oxygen using an appropriate oxygen delivery mask.
   2. Consider ALS back-up
          • Consider the requirement for ALS back-up as per Universal Care CPG.
          • ALS back-up should be called if:
                  • The patient is hemodynamically unstable. Or/

                   • The patient is in VT, or is having ‘runs’ of VT. Or/
                   • The patient has severe difficulty of breathing. Or/
                   • The patient is agitated to a level which inhibits patient care. Or/
                   • The EMS team suspect the patient is likely to deteriorate into cardiac arrest.
   3. ACS General Management
           • If patient presents with ischemic type chest pain (see table below) or meets the ACS inclusion Criteria
           • Consider Aspirin
                • Administer Aspirin 162-324mg PO.
                • The standard aspirin dose is 324mg (4 tablets)
                • If the patient has taken aspirin within the last 60 minutes:
                       • Confirm the dose they have received.
                       • Administer additional Aspirin to reach a maximum total dose of 324mg.
           • Consider GTN
                • If ischemic chest pain is present, administer GTN.
                • GTN 0.4mg SL, repeat every 3-5 mins while symptoms persist and if systolic BP remains ≥100 mmHg.
                  (max 1.2mg)
                • Reassess vital signs before repeating dose.
                     • Note Contraindications: Phosphodiesterase inhibitor use (typically used for erectile dysfunction),
                       inferior STEMI with suspected right ventricular involvement.
           • Consider Morphine
                • Indicated for severe pain only.
                • Administer Morphine IV/IO 1-2mg increments, repeat every 5-10 minutes, maximum 10mg.
           • If STEMI on 12-Lead ECG:
                • Apply AED Pads
                       • In preparation for cardiac arrest
                • Continuous ECG Monitoring
                       • Routinely use 4-lead ECG monitoring as well as applying the pads so that pacing and electrical
                          cardioversion can be easily performed.
                • Expedite transport
                       • Expedite transport and prioritize transfer of the patient to hospital—however, do not neglect proper
                          patient assessment and management.
                       • Coordinate with Dispatch 2 to ensure that the patient is transferred to an appropriate cardiac
                          hospital.
           • If Non-STEMI on 12-Lead ECG:
                • Perform serial 12-leads (take a 12-lead with each set of vitals) to observe for progression.
                • Identification of Occlusion Myocardial Infarction (OMI):
                     • OMI can indicate acute coronary occlusion requiring urgent intervention despite the absence of
                       classic STEMI criteria—contact Telemedicine consultation for confirmation and escalation if
                       required.
                     • Recognize Occlusion Myocardial Infarction (OMI) that may not present as STEMI on ECG; look for
                       subtle signs like hyperacute T-waves, reciprocal changes, or ST depression in multiple leads.
   4. If Patient Hemodynamically Unstable
           • Consider Normal Saline Fluid Bolus
                     • If sBP <90mmHg:
                          • Consider Normal Saline 250ml IV/IO
                          • Monitor closely for fluid overload/pulmonary edema.
                          • Stop fluid bolus if pulmonary edema develops.
                          • Fluid Bolus may be repeated as required.
   5. Contacting Telemedicine
           • In 12-Lead ECGs where the diagnosis is non-specific or unclear or if the clinician is unsure, telemedicine
             should be consulted for confirmation or second opinion.

          • Telemedicine should be used routinely by EMTs when the diagnosis is not clear STEMI.
   6. Patient Reassessment
          • Frequently Reassess the patient as per Universal Care CPG.
          • Continuous ECG monitoring is required for all patients with cardiac chest pain.


Patient Safety Considerations
   • Investigate uncommon indicators of cardiac chest pain, including pain radiating to arms or jaws, dizziness,
     sweating, vomiting, and nausea.
   • Phosphodiesterase inhibitors (such as Viagra) may be used for other conditions—closely investigate the patient’s
     medications.
   • GTN should be avoided in tachy and brady arrhythmias; management of the rate should be prioritized.
   • Administer fluids cautiously; small amounts of fluid in patients with cardiac failure may result in fluid overload.

Documentation
   • Identification: Full patient name, age, and gender.
   • Mechanism / Medical Complaint: Presenting symptoms (e.g., chest pain, radiating pain), initial vital signs.
   • Injuries / Information: Relevant history (e.g., cardiac risk factors, medications).
   • Signs:
         • Full vital signs including pulse, respiratory rate, SpO2, sBP.
         • ECG findings (e.g., STEMI, OMI indicators).
   • Treatment:
         • Oxygen administration, Aspirin, GTN, Morphine, and NaCl doses.
         • All interventions: Pads application, ECG monitoring, and response.
         • Justification if interventions were withheld.
   • Allergies: Documented allergies and drug interactions.
   • Medications: All pre-existing medications and any administered in the field.
   • Background: Relevant medical history, comorbidities, and recent procedures.
   • Other:
         • Communication with Telemedicine (time and instructions).
         • Response to interventions.
         • Consideration of reversible causes and actions taken.


Key Performance Indicators
   1. All patients with cardiac chest pain should receive a 12-lead ECG.
   2. Administer aspirin to all ACS patients (where appropriate).
