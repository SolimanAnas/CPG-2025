CPG 7.1            General Trauma Management

Patient Care Goals
    1. Rapidly identify and manage life-threatening injuries in trauma patients to prevent mortality and minimize
       morbidity.
    2. Stabilize patients through targeted interventions, including hemorrhage control, airway management, and shock
       prevention, while prioritizing rapid transport to a trauma center.
    3. Ensure safe and systematic care delivery, integrating spinal motion restriction, pain management, and targeted
       care delivery to optimize outcomes.

Patient Inclusion & Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion Criteria
    • Patients with a traumatic mechanism of injury.
    • Patients with traumatic injuries from blunt, penetrating, or blast mechanisms, including:
          • External or internal hemorrhage (e.g., lacerations, gunshot wounds, or blunt abdominal trauma).
          • Airway or respiratory compromise (e.g., facial fractures, chest trauma).
          • Neurological deficits or altered mental status (e.g., traumatic brain injury, spinal cord injury).
          • Fractures, dislocations, or soft tissue injuries causing pain or instability.
Exclusion Criteria
    • Patients with no evidence of trauma

Key Patient Assessment Points
   1. Mechanism of Injury
         • Identify the type and severity of trauma (e.g., blunt, penetrating, or blast) to predict injury patterns.
         • Assess kinetic energy involved (e.g., speed of vehicle, height of fall, or weapon type) to prioritize occult injuries.
   2. Primary Survey
         • Life-Threatening Injury Identification
               • Detect immediate threats using the C-ABC algorithm (Catastrophic Hemorrhage, Airway, Breathing,
                 Circulation).
               • Focus on catastrophic bleeding, airway obstruction, tension pneumothorax, or signs of shock (e.g.,
                 pallor, delayed capillary refill).
   3. Neurological and Spinal Assessment
         • Evaluate for traumatic brain injury (TBI) using Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) or AVPU, noting pupil asymmetry or
           posturing.
         • Assess for spinal injury risk (e.g., midline tenderness, neurological deficits) to guide spinal motion restriction
           (SMR).
   4. Rapid Trauma Survey
         • Perform a rapid head-to-toe assessment to identify additional injuries (e.g., fractures, open wounds, or
           deformities).
         • Check for signs of internal injuries (e.g., abdominal rigidity, chest crepitus, or pelvic instability).
   5. Determine Triage Category
         • Determine Triage Category as per Patient Triage Categories CPG.


Patient Management & Interventions
    1. Consider ALS Back-Up
         • Request for ALS back-up as per Universal Care CPG.
              • Consider the requirement for narcotic analgesia.
    2. Primary Survey
         • Ensure scene safety, don personal protective equipment (PPE), and wear high-visibility apparel if needed.
         • Apply the C-ABC algorithm:
              • Catastrophic Hemorrhage / C-Spine Control:
                  • Control life-threatening external bleeding with tourniquets, pressure or hemostatic dressings.
                  • Maintain manual stabilization of c-spine if mechanism of injury suggests c-spine injury.

              • Airway:
                  • If airway obstruction or altered mental status results in the inability to maintain airway:
                       • Secure the airway with jaw thrust if cervical spine injury is suspected.
                       • See the Airway Management CPG.
              • Breathing:
                  • Consider Tension Pneumothorax
                       • If tension pneumothorax present, perform Chest Needle-Decompression.
                            • Indications:
                                • Chest trauma with tracheal deviation
                                • Decreased breath sounds on affected side
                                • Hypotension
                  • If Open/Sucking Chest Wounds
                       • Apply Chest Seal to open chest wounds.
                  • Administer Oxygen
                       • Titrate oxygen to maintain Sp02 >94%.
                  • Ventilation Support
                       • Support inadequate ventilations with BVM ventilations.
              • Circulation:
                  • Consider Pelvic Fracture
                       • If pelvis is unstable, apply pelvic binder.
                  • Establish large bore IV/IO access.
                       • Consider IO Access if IV access is delayed or not feasible.
                  • Consider IV/IO fluid administration (see below).
   3. Rapid Trauma Survey
        • Conduct a systematic detailed head-to-toe examination physical to identify non-life-threatening injuries (e.g.,
          fractures, lacerations).
   4. Fluid Resuscitation
        • Note there are additional fluid resuscitation targets for Traumatic Brain Injuries (below)
        • Adults:
               • If SBP is less than 90 mmHg or HR greater than 120 BPM:
                         • Consider 500 mL bolus of Normal Saline or Lactated Ringers IV/IO.
               • If SBP greater than 90 mmHg and heart rate less than 120 BPM, no IV fluids are required.
               • Repeat 500mL IV fluid bolus’ as needed to maintain a SPB of 90mmHg
               • Maximum total IV/IO fluid bolus 2000mL.
        • Pediatrics:
               • If tachycardic for age, AND signs of poor perfusion (hypotension for age, greater than 2-second capillary
                 refill, altered mental status, hypoxia, weak pulses, pallor, or mottled/cool skin):
                         • Administer 20 mL/kg IV/IO crystalloid bolus and reassess.
                         • Repeat 20 mL/kg IV/IO fluid bolus’ as needed if signs of shock remain.
                         • Maximum total IV/IO fluid bolus of 60mL/kg.
               • Fluid administration target is normal blood pressure for patient age.
   5. Traumatic Brain Injury Fluid Administration Target
        • If Traumatic Brain Injury is identified:
               • Administer IV/IO fluid bolus as above to target:
                      • Adults: SBP of 120mmHg.
                      • Pediatrics: normal SBP for age.
   6. Consider Tranexamic Acid (TXA)
        • Indicated for:
               • Uncontrolled Bleeding, within 3 hours from time of injury.
        • Administer:
               • Adults: 1000mg IV/IO (diluted in 100ml normal saline and administered over 10 minutes)

              • Pediatric: 15mg/kg IO/IO diluted with normal saline to make a total of 20ml and infused over 10 minutes.
         • TXA should be administered as early as possible, ideally within 1 hour from time of injury.
         • Doses should be reduced if the patient is known to have renal impairment.
   7. Avoid Hypothermia
         • Remove wet clothing.
         • Cover the patient with a thermal (foil) blanket.
         • Attempt to limit the time the patient is uncovered for assessment/treatment.
   8. Secondary Survey
         • Conduct a systematic, detailed head-to-toe physical examination to identify non-life-threatening injuries (e.g.,
           fractures, lacerations).
         • Obtain OPQRST and SAMPLE history, focusing on mechanism, symptoms, and pre-existing conditions.
   9. Spinal Motion Restriction (SMR)
         • If patient meets criteria for SMR, follow Spinal Motion Restriction CPG.
   10. Pain Management
         • Assess for patient pain as per Pain Management CPG.
         • Administer analgesia as per Pain Management CPG.
   11. Fracture and Soft Tissue Management
         • Splint fractures or dislocations to reduce pain and prevent further injury.
         • Cover open wounds with sterile dressings to minimize infection risk.
   12. Patient Re-Assessment
         • Frequently Reassess the patient as per Universal Care CPG.
         • Reassess all vital signs and interventions (e.g. vital signs, AVPU, GCS, IV patency).
                • Red Priority: Every 5 minutes.
                • Yellow Priority: Every 10 minutes.
                • Green Priority: Every 15 minutes.
   13. Consider Red Flags
         • Uncontrolled hemorrhage or signs of hypovolemic shock (e.g., SBP <90 mmHg, tachycardia, delayed capillary
           refill >2 seconds).
         • GCS ≤8, unequal pupils, or posturing indicating severe TBI.
         • Respiratory distress or hypoxia (SpO2 <90%) suggesting chest trauma (e.g., pneumothorax, hemothorax).
         • Suspected spinal cord injury with neurological deficits (e.g., paralysis, sensory loss).
         • High-risk mechanisms (e.g., ejection from vehicle, blast injury) with occult injuries requiring trauma center
           evaluation.

Patient Safety Considerations
   • Rapid Transfer Requirements
        • Patients with major hemorrhage, hemodynamic instability, penetrating torso trauma, require rapid surgical
           intervention.
         • Minimize scene time scene time as much as possible (goal is under 10 minutes).
   • Elderly Patients (>65 years):
        • Increased risk of severe injury from low-energy mechanisms (e.g., ground-level falls) due to frailty or
           anticoagulation.
         • Use cautious fluid resuscitation and monitor for medication interactions (e.g., warfarin, aspirin).
   • Pediatric Patients (<13 years):
         • Use Broselow Tape for weight estimation and medication dosing to ensure accuracy.
         • Involve a parent or guardian during assessment to reduce anxiety and provide history.
         • Adjust SMR devices and splints for pediatric anatomy to prevent complications.
   • Transport Safety
         • Prioritize rapid transport for critical patients (red priority) while continuing interventions en route.
         • The safety of the EMS team while driving is paramount, safe driving is required to prevent injury.

Clinical Insights & Additional Guidance
   • Transport Considerations
         • Transport to a level 1 trauma center for critical patients (e.g., polytrauma, TBI, or hemorrhagic shock).
         • Stable patients with isolated injuries (e.g., single fracture) may be transported to a general hospital if no
           trauma center is required.
         • Consider Airwing for time-critical cases with ground transport >30 minutes.



Key Documentation Points
   • Identification: Patient's name, age, gender.
   • Mechanism / Medical Complaint: Description of mechanism of injury.
         • Falls: Estimated height
         • Traffic Accident: approximate speed, and any complications: roll-over, ejection, trapped (time), other
            deceased or Red Priority in same vehicle.
   • Injuries / Information: Describe all injuries found.
   • Signs: Primary survey abnormalities & vital signs.
   • Treatment: Interventions performed
         • Catastrophic Hemorrhage Control: Tourniquet, hemostatic agents used.
         • C-spine: Spinal Motion Restrictions Used.
         • Airway: Airway adjuncts (OPA/NPA) or interventions
         • Breathing: Oxygen/BVM support
         • Circulation: IV/IO access gained, amount of fluid administered.
         • Medications Administered.
   • Allergies: Any known medication allergies.
   • Medications: Recent medications or known medical history.
   • Background: Relevant medical history.
   • Other: Time incident happened.


Key Performance Indicators
   1.   Apply a tourniquet for patients with uncontrolled limb bleeding.
   2.   Apply a pelvic binder for patients with an unstable pelvis.
   3.   Apply a chest seal for patients with open or sucking chest wounds.
   4.   Request ALS back-up for patients with a GCS<8 of Systolic BP <90 and transfer time of more than 10 minutes.
   5.   On scene time of <10 minutes for red priority patients.
