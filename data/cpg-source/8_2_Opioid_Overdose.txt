CPG 8.2           Opioid Overdose

Patient Care Goals
   1.   Rapidly recognize and manage life-threatening effects of Opioid overdose
   2.   Support airway, breathing, and circulation while minimizing aspiration risk
   3.   Identify opioid toxicity and administer naloxone (Narcan)
   4.   Transport to definitive care for monitoring, or antidotal therapy
   5.   Prevent complications through appropriate monitoring and communication

Patient Inclusion & Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion Criteria
    • Suspected or confirmed intentional or unintentional opioid overdose symptoms including:
          • Altered mental status
          • Respiratory depression
          • Cardiovascular instability
          • Seizure or coma
          • History or evidence of opioid ingestion
Exclusion Criteria
    • Poisoning from industrial/environmental toxins (See General Toxicology Management CPG).
    • Ingestion of non-toxic substances with no clinical symptoms
    • Isolated alcohol intoxication (addressed in separate guideline)


Key Patient Assessment Points
   1. Scene Clues
        • Pill bottles, syringes, patches, empty blister packs
        • Bystander report, suicide note, known substance use disorder
   2. Airway & Breathing
        • Assess for snoring respirations, cyanosis, shallow breathing
        • Suspect opioid toxicity if RR <12 with pinpoint pupils
   3. Circulation
        • Bradycardia or hypotension (opioids, beta-blockers)
        • Tachycardia and hypertension (amphetamines, cocaine)
   4. Neurological Status
        • GCS <15, agitation, confusion, seizures, or unresponsiveness
        • Pupils: miosis (opioids), mydriasis (sympathomimetics or withdrawal)
   5. Clinical Indicators of Opioid Toxicity Assessment
        • Respiratory rate <12
        • Pinpoint pupils
        • Depressed LOC
        • Evidence of opioid use or paraphernalia
   6. High-Risk Indicator Assessment
        • Identify patients at higher risk of severe outcomes, respiratory arrest, or complications:
            • GCS <8
            • Respiratory rate <10 or apnea
            • SpO₂ <94% despite oxygen
            • Pinpoint pupils with poor respiratory effort
            • Suspected co-ingestants (e.g., alcohol, benzodiazepines, stimulants)
            • History of chronic opioid use or recent release from incarceration (high overdose risk due to loss of
               tolerance)
            • Pediatric ingestion; even a small dose of opioid can be fatal
            • Extended-release or long-acting opioid ingestion (risk of re-narcotization)
            • Suspected intentional overdose or suicide attempt
   7. SAMPLE History

   8. Determine Triage Category
       • Determine Triage Category as per Patient Triage Categories CPG.
             • Red Priority: Respiratory depression, GCS <8
             • Yellow Priority: Stable vitals, moderate symptoms (e.g., drowsy, hypotensive)
             • Green Priority: Alert, no current symptoms, accidental ingestion


Patient Management & Interventions
   1. Consider ALS back-up
        • Consider the requirement for ALS back-up as per Universal Care CPG.
        • ALS back-up should be routinely requested for:
             • GCS <8
             • Respiratory compromise
             • Seizures or significant cardiovascular effects
             • Suspected poly-drug ingestion
   1. Primary Survey
        • Perform Primary Survey as per Universal Care CPG.
             • Provide BVM ventilation if RR <10 or SpO₂ <94%
             • Suction as needed to clear airway from vomitus
   2. Oxygen Therapy
        • Administer high-flow O₂ for all patients with hypoxia or respiratory depression
        • Target SpO₂ ≥94%
   3. Opioid Overdose Management
        • Administer Naloxone:
               • Indications:
                     • Signs and Symptoms consistent with opioid overdose, and/
                           • Altered Mental Status, or
                           • Respiratory Depression, or
                           • Cardiovascular compromise.

   5. Transport Considerations
        • Transport all opioid overdose patients, even if they initially respond to naloxone
        • Provide early notification to receiving facility if airway or cardiovascular support is required
        • Notify dispatch and police in suspicion of drug overdose before transport


Patient Safety Considerations
   • Maintain airway protection at all times
   • Do not rely on response to naloxone alone to rule out other causes
   • Monitor for re-narcotization after naloxone wears off
   • Anticipate withdrawal-related agitation and ensure scene safety
   • Avoid judgmental behavior; overdose is a medical emergency
   • Use Broselow tape for pediatric dosing and airway sizing


Clinical Insights & Additional Guidance
   • Poly-substance overdose is common; always consider co-ingestants
   • Naloxone can precipitate withdrawal, especially in opioid-dependent patients
   • Pediatric opioid ingestion can be lethal even in small amounts; escalate early
   • If patient becomes aggressive after naloxone, ensure crew safety and request support
   • TeleEMS consultation recommended for complex cases or recurrent overdoses


Documentation
   • Identification: Name, age, gender, known substance use history
   • Medical complaint: Suspected overdose, opioid exposure
   • Information: Scene findings, time of ingestion, prior similar events
   • Signs: GCS, RR, HR, BP, SpO₂, pupil size
   • Treatment: Oxygen, BVM, naloxone route/dose/time
   • Allergies: To naloxone or other known agents
   • Medications: Prehospital interventions
   • Background: Psychiatric history, substance use disorder
   • Other: Family presence, police on scene, suicidal intent


Key Performance Indicators
   1. Administer naloxone for suspected opioid toxicity.
