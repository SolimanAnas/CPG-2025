M29                  Naloxone

 Scope of Practice       EMT

 Classification          • Opiate Antagonist / Opiate Antidote
 Usual                   • 0.4mg/1ml ampule
 Presentation            • 4mg pre-filled intranasal spray
 Mechanism of            • Naloxone competitively binds with opiate receptors reversing the effect of opiate
 Action                    agonists such as morphine.

 Indications             • Opiate overdose


 Contraindications       • Known allergy


 Precautions             • Severe hypoxia prior to administration

                         • Withdrawal symptoms (including arrhythmias and seizures)
 Side Effects
                         • Hypoxia-related agitation after rapid reversal without pre-oxygenation


 Adult Dose              • 0.4mg IV/IO/IM (repeated every 3 minutes as required, maximum 2mg)
 & Administration        • 4mg IN (repeated every 3 minutes as required)


 Pediatric Dose
                         • 0.01mg/kg IV/IO/IM (repeated every 3 minutes as required)
 & Administration
                         • Pregnancy: Safety has not been established. However, naloxone should be
 Use in Pregnancy          administered when clinically indicated.
 and Lactation           • Lactation: Safety has not been established. However, naloxone should be
                           administered when clinically indicated.
                         • IV or IN is the preferred method of administration
                         • All cases of opiate overdose should be transferred to hospital even if naloxone has
                           been effective.
                         • If maximum dose has been reached, strongly consider other causes of
 Additional
                           unconsciousness before further administration.
 Information
                         • For intranasal pre-filled spray:
                                 • Do not ‘test’ or ‘prime’ before use.
                         • Titrate naloxone carefully to reverse respiratory depression only, while monitoring
                           to prevent fully awakening the patient which may cause excessive aggression.
