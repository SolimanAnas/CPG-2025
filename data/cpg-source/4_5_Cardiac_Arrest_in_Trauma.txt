CPG 4.5            Cardiac Arrest in Trauma

Patient Care Goals
    1. Rapidly identify and manage traumatic cardiac arrest.
    2. Perform high-quality resuscitation (CPR) and specific interventions related to traumatic arrest to maximize
       survival.
    3. Ensure safe transport to a trauma center.

Patient Inclusion & Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion Criteria
    • Adult and pediatric patients presenting with traumatic cardiac arrest, including:
            • Unresponsiveness with no signs of life (e.g., no pulse, no breathing, or agonal gasps) following significant
              trauma.
Exclusion Criteria
    • Non-traumatic cardiac arrest patients.


Key Patient Assessment Points
    1. Traumatic Cardiac Arrest Confirmation
           • Perform Primary Survey as per Universal Care CPG.
               • Verify unresponsiveness by assessing for no response to verbal or painful stimuli (AVPU).
               • Check for absence of pulse (carotid or femoral) for 5-10 seconds.
               • Confirm absence of normal breathing or presence of agonal gasps for 5-10 seconds.
    2. Trauma Mechanism Assessment
           • Identify the mechanism of injury (e.g., blunt vs. penetrating, location of trauma such as chest, abdomen, or
             head).
           • Assess for high-risk injuries likely causing arrest (e.g., tension pneumothorax, massive hemothorax, or
             cardiac tamponade).
    3. Signs of Irreversible Death
           • In the unwitnessed traumatic cardiac arrest patient, identify signs of irreversible death, including:
               • Decapitation
               • Incineration
               • Body decomposition
               • Rigor mortis
    4. Futile Resuscitation in Trauma Assessment
           • In the unwitnessed traumatic cardiac arrest patient, assess for signs which indicate that resuscitation is not
             indicated. Including:
                    • Victim of blunt or penetrating trauma
                    • No spontaneous movement
                    • No pupillary response
                    • Apneic and pulseless
                    • ECG shows no organized rhythm
           • All signs must be present to not start resuscitation (CPR)
    5. Determine Triage Category
           • Determine Triage Category as per Patient Triage Categories CPG.
               • All patients cardiac arrest are Red Priority.


Patient Management & Interventions
    1. Deciding to Start CPR
          • Witnessed Cardiac Arrest
                  • If the Cardiac Arrest was witnessed by ambulance staff, CPR should be initiated.
          • Unwitnessed Cardiac Arrest
              • Signs of Irreversible Death Assessment:
                    • If any of the signs below are present, resuscitation (CPR) is not required. Including:

                       • Decapitation
                       • Incineration
                       • Body decomposition
                       • Rigor mortis
               • Futile Resuscitation in Trauma Assessment:
                     • If all of the below signs are present, resuscitation (CPR) is not required, including:
                             • Victim of blunt or penetrating trauma
                             • No spontaneous movement (Unresponsive on AVPU scale)
                             • No pupillary response (Pupils non-reactive to light).
                             • Apneic and pulseless
                             • ECG shows no organized rhythm (asystole).
   2. Request for ALS Back-up
          • Consider the requirement for ALS back-up as per Universal Care CPG.
          • If resuscitation is started ALS back-up should be considered.
          • Transfer to hospital should be prioritized, do not wait on scene, meet the ALS unit en route.
   3. Perform Resuscitation (CPR)
          • Perform resuscitation as per Adult or Pediatric Cardiac Arrest CPG.
   4. Consider Specific Cardiac Arrest in Trauma Interventions
          • Consider placing a pelvic binder performing bi-lateral chest needle decompression.
                 • Indications:
                       • Where the cause of the arrest is not clear (such as in polytrauma in a road traffic accident)
                         application of both the pelvic binder and performing bi-lateral chest needle decompression
                         should be considered.
                 • Contraindications:
                       • Where there is a clear injury which will not result in pelvic fracture or pneumothorax (such as
                         arterial limb bleeding, or isolated traumatic brain injury).
          • The pelvic binder should be placed before chest compressions start – the time *permitted to place the
            pelvic binder is limited to 30 seconds after no pulse is felt.
          • Active external bleeding should be identified and immediately controlled using direct pressure, wound
            packing, or tourniquets as appropriate.
                 • Uncontrolled hemorrhage is a common reversible cause of traumatic cardiac arrest and must be
                   addressed rapidly.

Patient Safety Considerations
   • Resuscitation (CPR) should be initiated in cases when the Mechanism of Injury does not correlate with the clinical
     condition, suggesting a non-traumatic cause of the arrest.

Clinical Insights & Additional Guidance
   • For guidance on stopping or transferring CPR cases in trauma, see the Starting, Stopping or Transferring CPR CPG.
   • Tranexamic Acid (TXA) in Traumatic Cardiac Arrest
       • General Guidance:
           • TXA is not routinely indicated in patients who are already in cardiac arrest unless there is a high suspicion
             that the arrest is due to massive hemorrhage and ROSC (Return of Spontaneous Circulation) is achievable.
       • Indications for TXA in Traumatic Cardiac Arrest:
           • If the traumatic cardiac arrest is witnessed, and/
           • There are signs of massive hemorrhage (e.g., penetrating trauma, major limb injury, severe pelvic fracture).
           • TXA 1g IV/IO can be administered as early as possible, ideally within the first 3 hours of injury.
       • Contraindications of TXA in Traumatic Cardiac Arrest:
           • In unwitnessed arrest with prolonged downtime, or when death is clearly non-survivable (e.g., massive head
             injury, decapitation, hemicorporectomy).
           • If hemorrhage is not suspected to be the cause of arrest (e.g., isolated TBI).

Documentation
   • Identification: Patient’s full name, age, and gender.
   • Mechanism / Medical Complaint: Mechanism of injury (blunt, penetrating), suspected traumatic cardiac arrest.
   • Injuries / Information: Injury pattern noted, signs of irreversible death, resuscitation criteria met or unmet.
   • Signs: Initial rhythm, unresponsiveness, pulse and breathing status, GCS if regained, pupil response.
   • Treatment:
            • CPR and trauma-specific interventions: pelvic binder placement, bilateral chest decompression, any
              methods used to stop active bleeding.
            • IV/IO access, airway management, medications if applicable.
   • Allergies: Known drug or environmental allergies, if obtainable.
   • Medications: Pre-arrest medications if known.
   • Background: History if available from bystanders, caregivers, or ID.
   • Other: ECG rhythm strips, time CPR started/stopped, transfer or termination rationale.


Key Performance Indicators
   1. Immediately initiate CPR for traumatic cardiac arrests witnessed by EMS>
   2. Request for back up (where appropriate) for all traumatic cardiac arrests.
